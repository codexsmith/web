# Schemathematics Formal-Program Representation Contract

## Purpose

Schemathematics is a formal research program, not a generic article and not a novelty claim produced by renaming established mathematics.

Its public representation should make the standing of each formal object and research claim inspectable. The interface must help a reader recover what an object is, which conditions make it admissible, which operations those conditions license, what remains invariant, which dependencies are required, and which claims remain hypotheses or empirical questions.

## Core invariant

> A formal-research interface must preserve the difference between established mathematics, a representation of that mathematics, a research hypothesis about the representation, and evidence that the representation improves a task.

## 1. Formal fidelity precedes representational convenience

Accepted mathematical definitions and required conditions are authoritative wherever they already solve the problem.

An operative profile may reorganize those facts for comparison or machine traversal. It may not silently weaken, strengthen, or replace the underlying definition.

## 2. The operative profile is a coordinate system, not a theorem

The Schemathematics operative profile exposes eight coordinates:

1. Entity
2. Admissibility
3. Transformations
4. Invariants
5. Closure
6. Boundary
7. Provenance
8. Defect / repair

These coordinates describe what should be recoverable from a representation. Their usefulness is a research question; the list itself does not prove that every formal object requires a novel Schemathematics formalism.

## 3. Structural dependency must be visible

When one formal structure is obtained by strengthening another, the representation should expose:

- the base object,
- the required conditions,
- the added condition,
- the promoted structure,
- the newly licensed or guaranteed operations.

A reader should be able to reconstruct why the distinction matters rather than merely memorize two names.

## 4. Established examples remain established mathematics

The monoid → group comparison uses standard algebra as a witness for the representation method.

Universal invertibility is the added condition in the retained example. The interface may show the resulting guarantees—such as cancellation, reversible multiplication, and unique solutions to the displayed equations—but must not present those facts as Schemathematics discoveries.

## 5. Missing guarantees remain first-class information

A useful formal representation exposes not only what is available but also what is not guaranteed under the current conditions.

The monoid side therefore retains its explicit non-guarantees. Promotion to group does not retroactively imply that those guarantees existed in the weaker structure.

## 6. Research hypotheses remain hypotheses

The public program includes hypotheses about:

- indexing formal objects by operative profile,
- asymmetries between operationally equivalent representations,
- cross-domain matching by operative structure,
- possible gains in mathematical navigation and machine reasoning.

These must remain visibly marked as hypotheses until supported by appropriate formal or empirical evidence.

## 7. Proof obligations and benchmark obligations are different

Formal fidelity and logical consequences may be checked mathematically.

Claims that an operative representation improves retrieval, pedagogy, theorem proving, navigation, or mathematical AI require comparative benchmark evidence.

A worked example is not a benchmark result.

## 8. Equivalence burden is explicit

If established category theory, type theory, topology, algebra, logic, formal methods, knowledge representation, or another existing formalism already carries the required structure, Schemathematics must reduce to or interoperate with that machinery rather than duplicate it under new vocabulary.

Reduction to established machinery is a successful outcome when it preserves the needed structure.

## 9. Representation is not novelty

Making an established distinction easier to inspect, compare, teach, or compute can be useful without implying priority for the underlying mathematics.

The interface must never convert representational usefulness into a novelty claim by presentation alone.

## 10. Claim standing remains visible

The formal-program surface should distinguish at least:

- established mathematics used as source material,
- current Boundary First Labs research framing,
- research hypotheses,
- validated formal consequences where present,
- empirical performance claims requiring benchmarks,
- claims explicitly not yet established.

## 11. Responsive projection preserves dependency order

A wide formal atlas may show a dependency trace spatially.

A narrow representation may linearize that trace, but it must preserve the order:

base object → required conditions → weaker structure → added condition → promoted structure.

Responsive layout must not collapse dependency into decorative cards whose relation is ambiguous.

## 12. Accessibility preserves formal distinctions

Forced-colors and other accessibility projections may remove material effects, but they must retain:

- section hierarchy,
- dependency order,
- hypothesis labels,
- positive vs non-established claim standing,
- return-to-object navigation.

## Reusable formal-research grammar

The Schemathematics surface establishes a reusable third-layer grammar for formal programs:

**object vocabulary → definition boundary → dependency trace → worked construction/comparison → hypotheses → proof/validation burden → claim standing**

The exact renderer may change for another formal domain. The standing distinctions may not.
