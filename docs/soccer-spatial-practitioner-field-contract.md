# Boundary-First Soccer Spatial Practitioner Field Contract

Boundary-First Soccer is a practitioner doctrine and research on-ramp. Its specialized third-layer representation must help a reader inspect how a team state changes without allowing Boundary First terminology to replace established soccer knowledge, observation, or evidence.

## Core invariant

> A spatial practitioner surface must connect an action to the team state it creates or preserves, while keeping established domain observation and analysis authoritative over the explanatory vocabulary.

## 1. The field is relational, not empty geometry

Relevant space is determined by reachability, timing, orientation, pressure, support, cover, responsibility, and continuation. The surface may use field geometry, but it must not imply that spatial coordinates alone determine tactical value.

The six current-state lenses remain distinct:

- Access
- Support
- Pressure
- Orientation
- Promotion
- Repair

## 2. Territory is not progress

Forward ball motion is not automatically promotion. Backward or lateral circulation can be constructive when it repairs support, changes pressure orientation, reopens access, or creates a more consequential next state.

The worked backward-pass phase therefore remains a repair-and-promotion example, not a doctrine that backward passing is inherently good.

## 3. Possession is read as an ordered state transition

The five passes remain an ordered practitioner method:

1. Bound the phase.
2. Map access and support.
3. Read authored pressure.
4. Promote the advantage.
5. Repair before collapse.

Responsive projection may change geometry but must preserve this sequence.

## 4. Pressure is authored future constraint

Pressure is represented by the options an opponent removes, delays, or makes expensive and by the future action the opponent is trying to force. A visual pressure region must not be interpreted as a measured probability or tracking-derived pressure value unless such evidence exists.

## 5. Support is continuation, not proximity

A nearby teammate is not automatically useful support. Support means useful continuations survive after reception under the actual pressure, orientation, and timing constraints.

## 6. Candidate tags remain observational hypotheses

The tagging vocabulary is a candidate analyst grammar. Tags such as `pressure authored`, `support restored`, `advantage promoted`, or `transition defect` must describe observable team-state changes.

The interface must not present the tags as a validated ontology, replacement event schema, or superior analysis system merely because they are internally coherent.

## 7. Illustrative fit is not validation

The worked phase remains constructed teaching material. It is not a claim about a specific match, team, player, competition, or dataset.

Explanatory fit to familiar soccer situations does not establish predictive, coaching, or performance value.

## 8. Established soccer evidence remains the external witness

Validation can draw on:

- video;
- event data;
- tracking data;
- coaching interpretation;
- established tactical frameworks;
- inter-analyst agreement.

Boundary First terminology must import established football-analysis language whenever that language already captures the relevant structure.

## 9. Performance claims require comparative evidence

The current public standing does not establish that Boundary-First Soccer:

- improves team performance;
- improves player development;
- yields more reliable tags than established systems;
- infers pressure or promotion robustly without suitable observation;
- is necessary when conventional soccer concepts are sufficient.

## 10. Responsive and accessibility projections preserve semantics

Desktop may use a field-like spatial composition. Compact layouts must linearize the same state logic rather than compressing the pitch into illegibility. Forced-colors mode may remove material effects but must preserve hierarchy, sequence, standing, and the distinction between pressure, repair, promotion, and evidence.

## Acceptance statement

The specialized surface succeeds when a practitioner can answer four questions without reading it as a replacement tactical system:

1. What is the current team-state boundary?
2. Which options are being removed or preserved?
3. How does the candidate action change the next reachable state?
4. What observation would show that the analysis was right or wrong?
