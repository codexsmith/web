# Boundary First Law — provenance research representation contract

This contract governs the specialized third-layer representation for the Constitutional Law & Jurisprudence research program.

## Core invariant

A legal-research interface must make it easier to distinguish controlling or current authority, doctrinal analogy, proposed jurisprudence, law reform, empirical hypothesis, moral proposition, and open question than to confuse them.

The interface is a research and educational instrument. It is not a law firm surface, legal advice surface, litigation strategy surface, or substitute for current jurisdiction-specific primary legal research.

## Representation laws

### 1. Current law precedes proposed doctrine

The page begins from constitutional text and current doctrine. Boundary First proposals may be compared with those materials but may not be styled or described as if they are controlling authority.

### 2. Consequential propositions carry standing

A consequential legal proposition should expose its claim regime near the proposition itself. Status may not be relegated to a remote disclaimer when the main surface presents the proposition with authoritative visual weight.

The retained claim regimes are:

- constitutional text
- current doctrine
- doctrinal analogy
- proposed jurisprudence
- law-reform proposal
- empirical hypothesis
- moral / jurisprudential proposition
- open research question

### 3. Authority remains reconstructable

Current-law anchors carry source keys into an authority register. The register should prefer primary constitutional text and official legal sources where practical and must remain visibly distinct from analogy or intellectual-background material.

A source label is not itself proof that the cited material supports every downstream inference. Source provenance and interpretive reasoning remain separate layers.

### 4. Jurisdiction remains a boundary condition

Jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions require jurisdiction-specific research. A general systems pattern cannot silently become an assertion about the governing law of a particular dispute, program, court, deadline, right, remedy, or procedural posture.

### 5. Analogy is not doctrine

The due-process and Article III standing bridges are analytical comparisons. They may illuminate Boundary First concepts but may not imply that Boundary First consequence analysis is current due-process doctrine, Article III standing doctrine, or any other controlling legal test.

### 6. Proposal is not entitlement

The Consequential Actor concept, controlled-compression doctrine, Boundary First justification test, repair taxonomy, and jurisprudential maxims are research proposals or analytical systems unless separately established by current law.

They do not create rights, causes of action, defenses, remedies, or professional obligations by being represented on this site.

### 7. Procedure and consequence are separate closure surfaces

The legal process model may continue beyond judgment into consequence, witness, contest, repair, and learning. This is a systems-analysis representation, not a claim that current doctrine universally defines legal finality or remedy in those terms.

### 8. Synthetic demonstrations remain synthetic

The flagship administrative-eligibility example is fictional and educational. It must not be presented as the law of an actual benefits program, licensing regime, jurisdiction, or person's legal rights.

### 9. Repair taxonomy does not manufacture remedies

Reversal, restoration, compensation, rehabilitation, correction, containment, structural repair, and recurrence prevention are analytical repair classes. Whether a remedy is available is a question of actual law, jurisdiction, procedural posture, and professional legal analysis.

### 10. Research stops where professional application begins

Boundary First Labs may map authority, representations, evidence paths, consequence chains, responsibility paths, contest paths, repair paths, and candidate doctrine.

Qualified lawyers determine how that analysis interacts with controlling authority, current validity, jurisdiction, professional duties, procedural posture, litigation strategy, advocacy, and individualized legal advice.

The preferred collaboration posture is adversarial doctrinal review: tell the Lab where the legal map is wrong.

## Required visual surfaces

The specialized representation must preserve:

1. a prominent research-not-advice practice boundary directly after the hero;
2. an authority baseline with current-law anchors and visible source keys;
3. a claim-status crosswalk;
4. the complete legal-process systems chain;
5. visibly distinct current-doctrine, analogy, and proposal lanes;
6. the synthetic consequence/repair demonstration;
7. an analytical repair taxonomy with an availability boundary;
8. a repeatable legal-systems diagnostic;
9. an authority/source register;
10. a professional-counsel boundary;
11. a public claim firewall.

## Responsive projection

Responsive presentation may change geometry but not legal standing.

A narrow layout may linearize authority cards, claim regimes, doctrine bridges, process stages, and the synthetic case, but it must retain the same order, source keys, claim labels, proposal boundaries, and non-advice notice.

## Forced-colors projection

Forced-colors mode may remove material textures and accent colors. It must preserve structural grouping, authority/source relations, claim standing, practice boundaries, and focus visibility without relying on color alone.

## Anti-regression rule

This surface must not regress to:

- a raw retained JSON record;
- an independent landing mini-site outside the Boundary Frame;
- a modal legal-detail window;
- a generic card article that visually collapses current law and proposed doctrine;
- a legal-advice or lawyer-like presentation.

## Public claim ceiling

Safe public standing is limited to describing Boundary First Law as an independent research, jurisprudential, systems-analysis, institutional-design, and civic/legal-education program.

The representation must preserve that proposed principles are proposals, structural patterns do not themselves establish liability, powerful private actors are not automatically constitutional state actors, Boundary First consequence standing is not Article III standing, Boundary First classification analysis is not the current Equal Protection test, and nothing in the surface supplies individualized legal advice.
