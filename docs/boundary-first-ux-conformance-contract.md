# Boundary First UX Conformance Surface Contract

## Purpose

Boundary First UX is a representation and interaction standard. Its public third-layer surface should therefore demonstrate the standard's laws rather than merely describe them in prose.

The specialized surface remains a detail representation of the canonical publication object at:

`/publications/methods/boundary-first-ux?detail=record:boundary-first-ux`

The legacy `/boundary-first-ux` route is only a compatibility entrance.

## Core invariant

> One semantic world may admit many lawful projections, but the renderer does not own semantic truth.

Identity, provenance, consequence, containment, transition semantics, accessibility obligations, and closure state must remain legible while the visual representation changes.

## Representation laws

### 1. Every view is explicitly bounded

The surface must visibly distinguish what is inside the current representation from consequential structure that remains outside it.

Partiality is not a defect by itself. Hidden partiality is.

### 2. Identity survives transformation

Reveal, reframe, promote, and renderer changes are transformations of representation. They must not imply that the underlying object began existing when it became visible.

### 3. The flagship is an operation, not an illustration

The Green Project sequence must expose the interaction grammar in order:

Orient → Inspect → Reveal → Reframe → Stress → Trace → Promote → Repair → Closure.

The project timeline must remain visible when lifecycle context is promoted. Promotion enlarges the represented context; it does not replace history.

### 4. Closure is a state

Closure must not be represented as another world-action button. It is earned when the relevant paths reconcile under declared scope and conditions.

### 5. Renderer independence is visible

World / experiential, non-spatial instrument, and timeline renderers are alternate projections of one semantic state. The UI must show the semantic core as distinct from the renderer surfaces.

### 6. Physical metaphor carries semantics

Depth, motion, framing, rotation, and zoom may be used only where the operation has a declared semantic meaning. Decorative 3D, gamification, or cinematic motion does not become BFUX merely by resembling the visual language.

### 7. Accessibility is paradigm correctness

Keyboard operation, reduced-motion parity, screen-reader semantics, non-spatial alternatives, visible context/provenance, non-color-only meaning, and removal of motor-precision gates are lawful projections of the same semantic world.

Accessibility must not be framed as a degraded fallback copy.

### 8. Conformance is not a visual score

The ten conformance questions are inspectable obligations. The candidate levels:

- BFUX-informed
- BFUX-compatible
- BFUX-conformant

remain **proposed, not yet canonical**. The public surface must not imply certification, endorsement, or a stable scoring rubric.

### 9. Standard, demonstration, and laboratory remain distinct

The normative standard, reference demonstration, and experimental laboratory have different standing.

Experimental contact may produce evidence, defects, or reusable methods. It does not promote an object into standard or canon without the corresponding review gate.

## Required instruments

The specialized third layer must expose at least:

- explicit inside/outside representation boundary;
- five core principles;
- flagship nine-step interaction sequence;
- three-act grouping: Trust, Discovery, Reconstruction;
- project-to-lifecycle timeline proof;
- renderer-independence panel;
- semantic stack;
- world actions, workbench operators, and world events;
- closure-as-state rule;
- conceptual tangibility and motion laws;
- named interaction-pattern catalog;
- accessibility obligations;
- conformance questions and provisional levels;
- standard / demonstration / lab separation;
- explicit non-claims.

## Responsive law

Responsive behavior is itself a BFUX projection.

- Wide surfaces may show the action grammar, renderer family, conformance criteria, and timeline simultaneously.
- Intermediate surfaces may recompose banks into fewer columns while preserving semantic grouping.
- Narrow surfaces must become a single reading flow without losing sequence, type, inside/outside distinction, or conformance standing.
- Horizontal overflow is admissible only where the represented object is intrinsically ordered across an axis, such as the lifecycle timeline.

## Accessibility / forced colors

Decorative textures, shadows, and material effects may disappear in forced-colors mode. Boundary, hierarchy, action names, conformance standing, and return semantics must remain.

## Non-goals

This surface is not:

- a component library;
- a BFUX certification tool;
- a mandatory visual skin;
- a graph-only interface;
- a claim that 3D or animation are required;
- a replacement for established accessibility or design-system practice.

## Acceptance criteria

The specialized BFUX third layer is correct when:

1. `/boundary-first-ux` resolves into the canonical publication-owned detail surface;
2. the generic record renderer is bypassed;
3. the current representation boundary is visible before the long-form doctrine;
4. the flagship interaction sequence and lifecycle promotion are inspectable;
5. renderer independence is represented as one semantic core with multiple projections;
6. closure is presented as state, not world action;
7. accessibility is presented as lawful projection parity;
8. provisional conformance levels are visibly labeled proposed rather than canonical;
9. standard, demonstration, and lab standing remain distinct;
10. desktop, mobile, and forced-colors representations preserve the same semantic distinctions.
