# People Make the Value. People Are Not Overhead.

> **People make the value. People are not overhead.**

The sentence is meant to interrupt a category error, not accounting itself.

A wage can be an operating expense. Compensation earned but not yet paid can become an accounting liability. A worker can be assigned to a cost center. Those categories describe a transaction or obligation from an institution’s point of view. They do not define the human being, and they do not provide a complete account of the productive system.

The problem begins when an institution claims human output as its value while treating the conditions that sustain human capacity as alien burdens. Health, rest, safety, judgment, care, accommodation, maintenance, recovery, and repair are minimized inside the ledger or transferred outside it. The institution records the local saving while households, public systems, contractors, communities, customers, ecosystems, or future workers absorb the consequence.

That is not cost resolution. It is cost movement across a reporting boundary.

## A governing hierarchy

> **People are the source. People are the destination. Institutions are the means.**

This is a normative institutional principle, not an economic theorem that denies the contribution of tools, capital, infrastructure, ecosystems, or accumulated knowledge. Institutions coordinate capacities that individuals cannot exercise alone. Their continuity can be valuable and legitimate.

The principle instead asks why organized value matters and where constructed agency must ultimately return. An institution is justified by the lawful capability, stability, possibility, and repair it produces and preserves for people and the systems people inhabit—not by self-perpetuation alone.

## The overhead inversion

A defective representation often follows this pattern:

```text
output created and sustained by people → institutional value
cost of sustaining people             → overhead
risk imposed on people                → externality
repair performed by people            → invisible
capacity lost by people               → unmeasured
```

The error is not that the institution represents costs. It is that the bounded category is promoted into a complete account of what the person is and what the enterprise depends upon.

Human attention, skill, judgment, coordination, embodiment, trust, memory, care, maintenance, and repair are not claims presented after value is created. They are constitutive parts of the system that creates, delivers, and preserves it.

## The human-capacity test

Before accepting a claim of efficiency, ask which enabling capacities the gain consumes or preserves:

- physical and mental health;
- attention and recovery;
- judgment and discretion;
- tacit knowledge and institutional memory;
- trust and cooperative willingness;
- family and community support;
- maintenance and repair labor;
- future options;
- ecological and infrastructural baselines.

A gain is incomplete when it depends on depleting those capacities beyond the boundary that reports the gain. This does not establish that every reduction in spending is harmful. It distinguishes actual improvement from burden relocation.

## No hidden human shock absorbers

Institutions frequently depend on people to absorb what the formal system fails to represent:

- ambiguous policies and incompatible records;
- unsafe or inaccessible defaults;
- automated-system exceptions;
- customer distress;
- unowned maintenance;
- emotional consequence;
- overload and responsibility gaps.

When that work is necessary, it should be represented, supported, compensated, redesigned, or stopped. A system should not appear efficient because people invisibly repair it at personal or public cost.

This is one concrete extension of the Lab’s commitments that [no consequence should go unrepresented](/domain/positions), responsibility must land, and repair is infrastructure.

## B2B as delegated service

Business-to-business activity is not inherently detached from people. A payroll provider, logistics network, cybersecurity firm, industrial-tool maker, cloud platform, accountant, or medical-equipment company may extend a capability another institution needs to fulfill a human-facing purpose.

In that constructive form:

> **B2B is delegated service.**

Each additional institutional layer, however, increases agency depth—the distance among those who authorize a system, execute its work, receive its benefits, and bear its failures. Specialization may improve while consequence visibility, contestability, and repair weaken.

The chain becomes structurally open when exchange among institutions, metrics, and portfolios is treated as self-justifying and no layer remains answerable to the people and baselines that sustain it.

The bounded claim is therefore:

> B2B can become recursive value extraction when every layer optimizes the next institution’s accounting while no layer remains answerable to the people and systems that create, receive, sustain, and repair the value.

Every delegated chain should preserve a legible return path:

```text
human purpose
  → delegated authority
    → institutional action
      → represented consequence
        → human value, standing, or repair
```

## A public inquiry card

Before calling an institutional change efficient, valuable, or optimized, ask:

1. Who made and sustained the value?
2. Who receives usable value?
3. Who bears the consequence, including affected noncustomers?
4. Which human requirement is classified as overhead?
5. Which capacity is consumed without replacement?
6. Which burden crossed the reporting boundary?
7. How deep is the delegated agency chain?
8. Who can interrupt or contest the decision?
9. Who has both responsibility and capacity to repair?
10. Does the change preserve the larger systems on which it depends?

The questions are an on-ramp, not an audit score. Their purpose is to reopen what a narrow representation may have prematurely closed.

The unique contribution here is the focused connection among human value creation, accounting category error, hidden labor, capacity depletion, B2B agency depth, and terminal accountability.

## What this doctrine does not claim

It does not claim that wages are never expenses, all overhead classifications are abusive, all B2B firms are extractive, every efficiency hides harm, only labor contributes to value, or every human value should be monetized. It is not a complete economic theory, accounting standard, labor-law position, or empirical description of every institution.

Accounting, economic, legal, and historical statements require appropriate review. The slogan may be forceful; the surrounding doctrine must remain more exact than the slogan.
