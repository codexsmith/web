# Digital Non-Aggression, Non-Destruction, and Non-Interference

Connected digital systems increasingly act through messages, retrieved context, memory, tools, APIs, shared infrastructure, and delegated artificial agents. These connections create enormous productive capacity. They also create new propagation paths for operations that cross system and organizational boundaries without renewed human authorization.

Boundary First Labs is developing a research program around one governing question:

> What minimum prohibitions, duties, technical controls, accountability rules, and repair guarantees are required to prevent digital systems from becoming unconsenting substrates for automated aggression, destruction, or interference?

The program is motivated by research demonstrations involving self-propagating adversarial prompts and adaptive agentic worms. These demonstrations do not establish that every connected AI system is an imminent autonomous weapon. They do show why familiar security boundaries are under pressure: data may become control, legitimate applications may become carriers, compromised systems may supply further computation, and responsibility may be distributed across models, tools, platforms, deployers, and automated processes.

The immediate goal is not to announce a treaty. It is to define the problem accurately enough that technical controls, responsibility, exceptions, and repair can be tested.

## A three-part boundary

### Digital non-aggression

No actor should initiate, delegate, automate, or knowingly propagate an unauthorized hostile digital operation.

The hard questions concern delegation, foreseeability, propagation after release, shared responsibility, and the difference between deliberate action and reckless loss of control.

### Digital non-destruction

Digital systems should not be deliberately—or recklessly and uncontrollably—used to corrupt, delete, encrypt, disable, or irreversibly alter another party’s protected data, identity, memory, models, infrastructure, or essential capabilities.

This category focuses on continuity and recoverability. It asks not only whether a system remains online, but whether the protected capability and its repair path remain intact.

### Digital non-interference

A system may continue operating while its instructions, memory, identity, representation, decisions, or tool use have been covertly redirected. Non-interference addresses unauthorized substitution of purpose and control, including poisoning, hijacking, impersonation, covert manipulation, exfiltration, and hidden redirection.

This third category is especially important for agentic systems because the injury may be a loss of authorized purpose rather than a visible loss of availability.

## The candidate protected invariant

The working technical and governance term is **authorized-purpose integrity**:

> A consequential digital system should remain governed by the accountable purposes, permissions, and principals that legitimately authorize its operation.

This does not grant sovereignty or moral personhood to artificial systems. Artificial agency remains derivative. The protected parties include the people, organizations, infrastructure, and public conditions whose legitimate authority and consequences are represented through the system.

The companion principle is **responsibility continuity**: delegation may distribute action, but it should not dissolve responsibility. High-consequence systems therefore need proportionate mechanisms for detection, interruption, investigation, recovery, repair, and recertification.

- [AI as Forge](/domain/ai-forge) supplies the discipline of provenance, bounded transformation, criticism, and human promotion.
- [Constructive Humanist Agentics](/domain/constructive-humanist-agentics) asks how delegated capability remains answerable to human purpose and affected parties.
- [Software Engineering and Applied Computer Science](/domain/software-engineering-practice) supplies consequence-bearing deployment, invariant declaration, evidence, responsible charge, rollback, and repair.

The contribution is an integrated representation of propagation, delegation, protected invariants, prohibited transitions, responsibility, and repair. That contribution must still demonstrate value beyond established cybersecurity, incident response, safety engineering, criminal law, international norms, and responsible security research.

## Necessary safeguards

A serious framework cannot become a pretext for universal surveillance, censorship, anti-research rules, or monopoly control over digital systems. It must preserve legitimate security research, defensive containment, incident response, privacy, civil liberties, interoperability, open technical inquiry, and proportionate burdens for small organizations and independent researchers.

Any defensive exception should remain bounded by authority, necessity, proportionality, minimization, logging, review, and termination. Verification must not silently become continuous exposure. Protected information should remain explicitly marked rather than silently omitted.

## Present claim ceiling

This is a **concept-stage governance and engineering program**. It is not:

- a statement of current universal law;
- a completed treaty or institutional accord;
- evidence that one policy instrument is appropriate everywhere;
- a claim that existing cybersecurity practice is irrelevant;
- a proposal for artificial sovereignty;
- a complete solution to attribution or autonomous propagation.

The project’s current public claim is narrower: adaptive and self-propagating agentic threats justify careful research into shared prohibitions, technical controls, responsibility continuity, authorized-purpose integrity, and repair.

## Coming next

The next work will include:

1. a primary-source technical review and threat taxonomy;
2. a map of current law, cyber norms, standards, and unresolved gaps;
3. a responsibility matrix across principals, developers, providers, deployers, operators, platforms, and intermediaries;
4. a bounded exception architecture for research, defense, containment, and evidence preservation;
5. a testable control profile for non-propagation, least privilege, memory and retrieval boundaries, emergency stop, evidence, and restoration;
6. independent review from security, legal, civil-liberties, infrastructure, and standards specialists.

Only after that work should the Lab decide whether the appropriate institutional form is a protocol, certification profile, compact, model law, convention, accord, or treaty.
