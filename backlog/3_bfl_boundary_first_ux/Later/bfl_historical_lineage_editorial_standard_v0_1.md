# Historical Lineage & Foundations Editorial Standard v0.1

**Status:** implementation and editorial guidance; all public citations require source verification.

## Purpose

This layer situates Boundary First Labs inside the established mathematical, physical, computational, philosophical, institutional, and engineering traditions that make the present work expressible. It is not a pantheon, a greatness-by-association device, or a claim that major historical figures would endorse the work.

## Governing rule

> Lineage identifies foundations, inherited problems, formal obligations, and critical comparisons. It does not confer authority.

## Works first, people second

Lead with the contribution, formalism, method, problem, experiment, or institutional tradition. Attach the person or institution as attribution.

Preferred:

- **Riemannian geometry** — Bernhard Riemann
- **Stored-program architecture and automata** — John von Neumann
- **Dirac operators and relativistic quantum formalism** — Paul Dirac
- **Noncommutative geometry and spectral triples** — Alain Connes
- **Black-hole radiation and horizon thermodynamics** — Stephen Hawking and the wider black-hole thermodynamics tradition

Avoid a row of famous portraits or names with no explanation of the inherited object.

## Mandatory lineage card

Every public card must answer:

1. **What did this work establish?**
2. **Why does it matter here?**
3. **How does the present program use or compare with it?**
4. **What is not being claimed?**
5. **What is the citation status?**

## Relation classes

- `documented-direct-influence`
- `foundational-formalism`
- `foundational-research-tradition`
- `methodological-lineage`
- `historical-precedent`
- `critical-counterpoint`
- `neighboring-research-lineage`
- `shared-problem`
- `independent-convergence`
- `comparative-reference`

Do not use `influencedBy` as a generic relation when the evidence only supports disciplinary foundation or editorial comparison.

## Bounded language

Preferred verbs:

- builds within
- draws upon
- is guided by
- uses the formalism of
- inherits a problem from
- develops alongside
- compares with
- reinterprets through
- extends a question raised by

Avoid unless independently demonstrated:

- completes
- solves
- supersedes
- unifies
- fulfills
- continues the legacy of
- stands alongside
- is the next

## Global non-claim

Every major lineage surface must carry this or equivalent language:

> These references identify foundational works, inherited formalisms, neighboring research traditions, and critical comparisons. They do not imply endorsement, equivalence, direct succession, or validation of Boundary First Labs.

## Separate intellectual lineage from corpus history

- **Lineage** explains established external foundations and neighboring traditions.
- **History** explains how the Boundary First corpus developed, changed names, split theories, revised claims, and replaced artifacts.

They must be separate tabs and separate edge types.

## Citation and evidence gates

Before promotion to public copy:

1. Verify the exact work and date.
2. Verify edition and translation where relevant.
3. Replace broad biography with a claim-specific contribution.
4. Establish whether the relation is direct influence, disciplinary foundation, or editorial comparison.
5. Add page-level citations when the local sentence depends on a specific formulation.
6. Preserve disagreements and alternative traditions.
7. Have a domain reviewer inspect the wording for major formal or historical claims.

## Anti-teleology rule

Do not render history as an inevitable march toward Boundary Theory. Show competing formalisms, unresolved disputes, critical counterpositions, and places where the current framing may add nothing.

## Portrait and visual-media rule

Portraits are optional and secondary. Diagrams, works, equations, instruments, and historical problems should carry the primary visual burden. No visual hierarchy should imply that a historical figure authorizes the present work.
