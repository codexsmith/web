## Goal

Create a **single-page overview of Boundary First Labs** that can be understood as a map of the whole institution rather than a catalog of projects.

This page should serve three jobs at once:

1. **Tour map** — the opening map for the five-minute guided tour.
2. **Standalone overview** — a single-sheet explanation that can be viewed independently.
3. **Compression test** — a deliberately constrained representation of the Lab that must preserve the institution's essential invariants without reproducing the corpus.

The difficulty is the point. Boundary First Labs contains research, methods, software, products, experiments, public-interest applications, scientific machinery, and multiple domain programs. A successful one-page overview must not attempt to enumerate all of that material.

It must preserve the **causal spine**.

## Core design rule

> **Do not summarize the corpus. Summarize the causal structure of the Lab.**

The page should answer, in one connected representation:

```text
WHAT ARE YOU?
    -> WHAT PROBLEM DO YOU SEE?
        -> WHAT IS YOUR CENTRAL INSIGHT?
            -> WHAT DO YOU DO DIFFERENTLY?
                -> WHAT MACHINERY COMES FROM THAT?
                    -> WHERE DOES IT APPLY?
                        -> WHY DOES IT MATTER?
```

The target causal spine is:

```text
DIFFICULT SYSTEMS
    -> hide consequential distinctions
        -> because representations determine what can be seen and done
            -> so expose the boundaries and preserve the important distinctions
                -> turn that into methods and machinery
                    -> apply that machinery across domains
                        -> increase capacity, agency, accessibility, and competence
```

That is the one-page Boundary First Labs representation.

# Boundary First Labs

### Software for difficult systems.

Boundary First Labs is a public-interest applied systems lab.

We study a simple but far-reaching problem:

**The way a system is represented determines what can be seen, reasoned about, changed, and acted upon.**

In complicated systems, important distinctions are often buried inside abstractions. Assumptions disappear into interfaces, models, procedures, institutions, and software — and those hidden assumptions eventually become operational consequences.

## The Boundary-First Move

Start with the distinctions that actually matter.

**Find the boundaries.  
Make them explicit.  
Determine what can cross them.  
Identify what must remain invariant.  
Build outward from there.**

This is the common move behind Boundary First research, engineering, software, and public-interest work.

## From Idea to Apparatus

**FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH**  
Boundary Theory, Distinction Space, Representational Mechanics, Schemathematics, and related work investigate boundaries, representations, admissibility, invariance, closure, defect, and transformation.

↓

**METHOD**  
Boundary-First Engineering and the Agentic Scientific Method turn those ideas into procedures for investigating, designing, testing, and repairing complicated systems.

↓

**MACHINERY**  
Executable representations, structured research systems, formal workflows, claim graphs, corpus machinery, interactive interfaces, and computational experiments make those procedures operational.

↓

**APPLICATIONS**  
The same machinery can be applied to software, artificial intelligence, scientific research, mathematics, education, law, civic systems, organizational processes, and other domains.

## One Loop, Many Domains

**MODEL → REPRESENTATION → OBSERVATION → ACTION → EVIDENCE → REVISION**

A representation is not merely a picture of a system.

It determines what the system — or the person using it — is capable of distinguishing.

Change the representation and you can change the problem that is possible to solve.

## What This Looks Like in Practice

**Research**  
Formalizing new ways to reason about boundaries, representations, systems, and computation.

**Engineering**  
Building software and interfaces that expose structure instead of hiding it behind unnecessary abstraction.

**Science**  
Treating formal and computational work as inspectable, testable experimental machinery.

**Public Interest**  
Applying the same methods where clearer systems can increase capacity, agency, accessibility, and public competence.

## Why One Lab Does All of This

The domains are different.

The underlying problem frequently is not.

Software, science, AI, law, education, institutions, and mathematics all depend on representations. All create boundaries. All admit some distinctions and suppress others. All transform information. All can fail when the wrong structure becomes invisible.

Boundary First Labs works on that shared layer.

## The Goal

**Make difficult systems more inspectable, testable, executable, understandable, and usable.**

Not by eliminating abstraction.

By making abstraction accountable to the structure it is supposed to preserve.

### If you remember one thing

**Representation is the control.**

What a system can distinguish constrains what it can know.

What it can represent constrains what it can do.

**Boundary First begins there.**

## Visual architecture

The final page should **not** be implemented as the prose draft above stacked from top to bottom.

The intended visual object is closer to a **single-sheet systems diagram / apparatus map**.

A strong high-level reading order is:

```text
WHY
  -> INSIGHT
      -> METHOD
          -> MACHINERY
              -> APPLICATION
                  -> PURPOSE
```

The visitor should be able to apprehend that sequence from the page composition before reading every sentence.

### Candidate central composition

Place the Representation Loop near the center as the shared mechanic.

Around it, connect a small number of regions:

```text
                    WHY
                     |
                     v
                [ INSIGHT ]
                     |
        +------------+------------+
        |                         |
        v                         v
     METHOD                    RESEARCH
        |                         |
        +------------+------------+
                     |
                     v
                 MACHINERY
                     |
          +----------+----------+
          |          |          |
          v          v          v
       SCIENCE    SOFTWARE   PUBLIC INTEREST
          \          |          /
           \         |         /
            +--------+--------+
                     |
                     v
                  PURPOSE
```

This is schematic only. The actual composition should follow the established Boundary First physical-apparatus language rather than resemble a generic flowchart.

### Navigation behavior

Every major region can double as a deeper route:

```text
THEORY
METHOD
RESEARCH
MACHINERY
PRODUCTS
PUBLIC INTEREST
```

The page therefore functions as both explanation and routing surface.

## Critical tour behavior: opening map / closing map

This is a primary requirement.

### Opening

At the beginning of the five-minute tour, show the visitor the entire single-page overview.

They do **not** need to fully understand it yet.

The page establishes the total shape of the machine.

### Guided traversal

The tour then uses that same representation as a navigation and attention surface.

Example sequence:

```text
"Boundary First Labs starts with a problem."
    -> illuminate WHY

"Our central claim is about representation."
    -> follow the route into INSIGHT / REPRESENTATION LOOP

"That produces a method."
    -> illuminate METHOD

"The method has been made executable."
    -> illuminate MACHINERY

"And the same machinery recurs across very different domains."
    -> illuminate APPLICATION branches
```

Where useful, the tour may move from this map into live site objects and then return to the map.

### Closing

At the end of the tour, show the **same complete single-page overview again**.

Do not substantially replace or simplify the map between opening and closing.

The intended effect is:

> **The representation did not change. The visitor's ability to read it did.**

This is unusually appropriate to Boundary First Labs and should be treated as a core experiential principle rather than an incidental animation.

The closing map should then expose deeper routes into the Lab.

## Compression principle

The single-page overview should itself be treated as a Boundary-First artifact.

Its design problem is:

```text
FULL LAB REPRESENTATION
    -> impose one-page boundary
        -> identify invariants
            -> preserve causal structure
                -> discard nonessential detail
```

The invariants to preserve are:

1. **Identity** — public-interest applied systems lab.
2. **Problem** — consequential structure becomes hidden through representation and abstraction.
3. **Insight** — representation governs observability and action.
4. **Move** — expose boundaries and preserve important distinctions/invariants.
5. **Method** — convert the insight into repeatable investigative and engineering procedures.
6. **Machinery** — make those procedures executable and inspectable.
7. **Recurrence** — the same structural problem appears across many domains.
8. **Purpose** — increase inspectability, competence, capacity, agency, accessibility, and usability.

Anything that does not materially support one of these invariants should be presumed removable from this particular representation.

## Anti-patterns

Do not turn the page into:

- a project inventory;
- a list of every theory name;
- an exhaustive domain taxonomy;
- a founder biography;
- a publication index;
- a giant wall of prose;
- an investor pitch deck compressed into one screen;
- a conventional About page;
- a generic node graph with dozens of unlabeled connections;
- decorative systems imagery with no explanatory job.

The page should remain legible to someone encountering Boundary First Labs for the first time.

## Relationship to the physical apparatus UX

Use the established clean physical language:

```text
institutional beige
+ restrained instrument construction
+ explicit routing
+ semantic connectors
+ physical hierarchy
+ controlled green signal accents
```

The map should feel like a **schematic plate attached to the machine** or an **overview panel for the machine itself**.

It can be information-dense, but the density must be systematic rather than busy.

A useful distinction:

> **Dense with relationships, sparse with decoration.**

## Responsive behavior

### Desktop

Aim for a true single-sheet overview where the major causal structure is visible at once.

The visitor should not need to scroll through seven independent content sections to understand the topology.

### Mobile

"Single-page" should mean **single conceptual map**, not necessarily literally one viewport height.

Preserve the relationships and traversal order rather than flattening the page into unrelated stacked cards.

Potential mobile techniques include:

- controlled zoom / focus;
- map-to-detail transitions;
- the existing mobile piping-reveal interaction pattern;
- a minimap or local-topology indicator;
- tour-driven focus that reveals only the currently relevant relation while preserving access to the whole map.

Do not solve mobile by deleting the relational structure that makes the page useful.

## Acceptance criteria

A first version is successful if:

- a new visitor can identify what Boundary First Labs is without reading the entire corpus;
- the page communicates one coherent causal spine rather than a catalog;
- the relationship `problem -> insight -> method -> machinery -> applications -> purpose` is visually legible;
- `Representation is the control` has an obvious conceptual home;
- the Boundary-First move is stated plainly;
- multiple domains are shown as recurrences of shared machinery rather than unrelated verticals;
- the page makes clear why research, engineering, science, and public-interest work coexist inside one Lab;
- the page is useful independently of the guided tour;
- the guided tour can use the page as an opening map;
- individual tour stops can illuminate/focus parts of the map;
- the same page can be shown again as the closing map;
- the closing experience demonstrates increased visitor comprehension without requiring a different diagram;
- major regions provide routes into deeper site material;
- the design remains compatible with the physical apparatus UX family;
- the desktop view reads as a coherent single-sheet representation;
- mobile preserves relational topology rather than degrading into a generic list.

## Short formulation

> **Build a single-page Boundary First Labs overview as the map of the whole machine. It should compress the Lab around the causal spine `difficult systems -> hidden distinctions -> representation -> Boundary-First method -> executable machinery -> cross-domain application -> increased agency/capacity`, serve as both the opening and closing map of the five-minute tour, and route visitors into deeper material without attempting to catalog the corpus.**
