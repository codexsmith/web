# Collaboration Doctrine

Boundary First Labs collaborates where there is a genuine division of comparative advantage.

We do not seek collaborators merely for association, visibility, or borrowed legitimacy. We seek people and institutions that possess something essential we cannot reasonably obtain, should not attempt to reproduce, or do not want to build ourselves: domain authority, practical experience, implementation capacity, distribution, infrastructure, institutional access, capital, or stewardship.

In return, we bring developed bodies of work, original representational systems, research methods, prototypes, and applications that our collaborators do not already possess and would face substantial uncertainty or expense in recreating independently.

A good collaboration therefore begins with two explicit questions:

**What does Boundary First Labs bring that the collaborator does not already have?**

**What does the collaborator bring that Boundary First Labs cannot or should not build alone?**

The purpose is not to merge identities or duplicate capabilities. It is to construct the smallest lawful relationship through which the work can be evaluated, improved, applied, distributed, funded, or placed with the party best equipped to carry it forward.

We remain flexible about form. A relationship may become expert review, advisory participation, co-development, licensing, publication, investment, acquisition, distribution, institutional sponsorship, or transfer of stewardship. We are format-agnostic, but not outcome-agnostic.

The required outcomes are:

* serious treatment of the work;
* clear attribution and provenance;
* preservation of the broader Boundary First method;
* fair recognition of each party’s contribution;
* and a structure that creates more value than either party could reasonably create alone.

Collaboration is not an admission of incapacity. It is a refusal to waste effort rebuilding strengths that already exist elsewhere.

## Pilot Discipline

The smallest useful collaboration is often a bounded pilot.

> **Bring us the system that almost works.**

A good pilot begins with a concrete defect, ambiguity, workflow, model, or decision surface that a partner already cares about. It does not begin by asking the partner to accept Boundary Theory in the abstract.

Every pilot should declare:

- one primary domain;
- one practical question;
- the partner's existing expertise and assets;
- the specific BFL instrument or representation being tested;
- the source and data boundary;
- the intended users or affected parties;
- the relevant evidence standard;
- success criteria;
- failure and stop criteria;
- expected deliverables;
- IP, confidentiality, and publication boundaries;
- a closeout and stewardship path.

The strongest pilot is one that can fail informatively.

A weak result, negative result, disagreement, or partner rejection is not hidden. It becomes evidence about where the instrument did not improve the map.

## Relationship Integrity

Boundary First Labs does not treat contact as validation.

Relationship language must remain exact:

- contacted;
- replied;
- met;
- advised;
- reviewed;
- supplied data or infrastructure;
- participated in a pilot;
- formally partnered;
- licensed;
- sponsored;
- endorsed.

These statuses are not interchangeable.

A narrow collaboration also does not imply endorsement of the founder, the entire institute, Boundary Theory as a whole, or an unrelated scientific claim. There is no hidden expansion from “this tool was useful here” to “the whole program is validated.”

## Repair Before Scale

Partnership growth is constrained by stewardship capacity.

Boundary First Labs should not multiply pilots, publications, products, or institutional promises faster than it can:

- answer collaborators;
- correct errors;
- preserve provenance;
- maintain released artifacts;
- protect confidentiality;
- honor attribution;
- record negative results;
- and close or transfer obligations responsibly.

Scale is useful only when the relationship remains legible enough for responsibility to land.


## Application-Specific Collaboration

Boundary First Labs develops methods and artifacts that may enter many different domains. It should not therefore attempt to become every kind of institution touched by its work.

A boundary-first framework for **chess** does not require BFL to become a chess media company, tournament organizer, curriculum publisher, or global training platform. It requires relationships with players, coaches, commentators, educators, publishers, platforms, and governing organizations capable of testing, teaching, distributing, and extending the framework.

A boundary-first framework for **soccer** does not require BFL to become a professional club, scouting organization, analytics vendor, or broadcast network. It requires access to coaches, players, analysts, academies, simulation environments, and competition data whose practical judgment can evaluate whether the representations survive contact with the game.

**Robotics and simulation** create a similar opportunity. RoboCup and related initiatives provide environments in which perception, coordination, planning, embodiment, communication, and emergent team behavior can be tested under explicit rules and measurable constraints. BFL can contribute representational mechanics, boundary definitions, comparative frameworks, and research questions without reproducing the full robotics infrastructure required to embody them.

**Pedagogy** requires teachers, schools, curriculum designers, learning scientists, and educational platforms. BFL may develop a clearer representational system or method of explanation, but experienced educators possess practical knowledge of sequencing, comprehension, classroom constraints, assessment, and learner variation that the lab should not pretend to independently replace.

**Weather** requires meteorologists, forecasters, observational networks, computational infrastructure, public agencies, emergency managers, and communities exposed to real consequences. BFL may contribute a new model of representation, uncertainty, boundary formation, or forecast communication. Validation and operational deployment, however, belong in partnership with institutions capable of comparing that model against physical data and established practice.

**News interpretation and media comparison** require another form of complementarity. An organization such as Ground News possesses distribution, source aggregation, user behavior, editorial experience, and an existing public interface for comparative representation. BFL may contribute a deeper account of framing, omission, boundary construction, representational asymmetry, and consequence. The potential relationship exists not because either organization is incomplete in general, but because each possesses a capability the other would find costly and uncertain to recreate.

Across these domains, the governing question remains the same:

**What is the smallest relationship that allows the work to encounter the strongest relevant reality?**

Sometimes that relationship is an expert review. Sometimes it is access to data or infrastructure. Sometimes it is a pilot program, research partnership, licensing agreement, educational trial, publication arrangement, sponsored implementation, or transfer to a more appropriate steward.

Boundary First Labs should build enough to establish the integrity, originality, and usefulness of the work. It should not automatically build every surrounding institution required to scale it.

The laboratory remains responsible for discovering, formalizing, and developing boundary-first systems. Its collaborators provide the specialized environments in which those systems can be tested, corrected, embodied, distributed, and sustained.

We collaborate not because we cannot proceed alone, but because proceeding alone would require us to become something the work does not need us to be.
