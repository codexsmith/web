Yes. Looking across these together, the important thing is that we do **not** actually have seventeen competing portfolios. We have several different projections of the same body of work, but they have been labeled “portfolio” because each was built for a different operational purpose.

The current material naturally resolves into a much cleaner public architecture.

### 1. The actual BFL work portfolio

These are the things Boundary First Labs can reasonably present as coherent **programs, methods, products, or substantial bodies of work**:

| Public program                                  | What belongs underneath it                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Boundary-First Engineering**                  | Closure-Driven Software Development, Boundary-First Software Toolkit, Boundary Failure Postmortem Lab, software/AI/ML consulting |
| **Corpus Forge / Research Infrastructure**      | Claim Ledger pipeline, Operator–Critic workflow, knowledge refinery, learning-navigation machinery                               |
| **Agency & AI Governance**                      | Agency Audit, Consequence-Bearing AI Deployment Kit, Semantic Closure Layer, authority matrices                                  |
| **Boundary-First Chess**                        | manuscript, doctrine, pedagogy, game analyses, PGN annotator/engine research                                                     |
| **Boundary-First Soccer & Multi-Agent Systems** | soccer doctrine, RoboCup program, tactical representations and simulation work                                                   |
| **Boundary-First Weather**                      | weather representation, simulation, risk communication, multiscale boundary research                                             |
| **Civilizational / Consequence Mechanics**      | consequence accounting, institutional analysis, public-agency work, externality and capacity-transfer cases                      |

That is substantially cleaner than the earlier six-item product map, although that map already had the right intuition: software, governance, chess, weather, outreach, and Research Forge were meant as different wedges into one common grammar. 

And importantly, **Chess has earned flagship status rather than “interesting example” status**: the partnership portfolio describes a complete 140-page draft manuscript, and its dedicated positioning document already identifies it as an accessible public wedge and proof of method.  

### 2. The “pilot portfolio” is not another portfolio

The six AI items are better understood as a **pilot menu beneath Agency & AI Governance**:

* Consequence-Bearing AI Deployment
* Participatory Agency Expectations
* Semantic Closure Layer
* Corpus Forge External Demonstration
* Boundary-Spectral Learning Benchmark
* Law-Following Agent Authority Matrix

That file itself describes them as bounded experiments with inputs, outputs, success criteria, and promotion gates. 

So a visitor should not encounter:

> Portfolio → AI Governance
> Portfolio → Pilot Portfolio
> Portfolio → Agency Audit

as though those were three peer institutions.

Instead:

> **Agency & AI Governance**
> Method: Agency Audit
> Available pilots: Deployment Audit · Semantic Closure · Authority Matrix · Participatory Expectations
> Case studies: eventually, completed external pilots

That immediately makes the lab look much more mature.

### 3. The partnership portfolio is a collaboration map

Likewise, Chess, Ground News/news representation, gender/public agency, Weather, Learning Navigator, Corpus Forge, Closure-Driven Software Development, RoboCup, and Agency Audit are explicitly framed as **opportunities to match an existing BFL asset to the right external environment through a bounded pilot**. 

That is not “our portfolio.”

It is:

> **Ways to Work With Us**

And the governing formula is already excellent:

> recognized problem → existing BFL asset → bounded pilot → declared evidence → next decision. 

That language belongs almost verbatim on the website.

I would also avoid publicly displaying prospective organizations such as Ground News, World of Wonder, RoboCup organizations, etc. as though they were current partners. The witness-domain portfolio is explicitly a graded testing strategy, not a list of existing relationships.  Publicly, we can describe the **domain** unless and until the relationship itself becomes real.

### 4. The comparative case portfolio is a research queue

Electric generation, refining, chemicals, AI data centers, public benefits, water extraction, infant formula, third places, housing financialization, and the eventual comparative atlas are not products either.

They are an unusually good **case-study research program**.

And that source is very disciplined about it: the cases are selected to test different transfer mechanisms, not to prove an omnibus accusation, and each case has explicit boundaries and evidence requirements. 

So the website might eventually say:

> **Consequence Accounting — Case Program**
>
> Studying how benefit, burden, capacity, maintenance, risk, and repair move across institutional boundaries.
>
> **Current case queue:** Energy · Data Centers · Labor/Public Benefits · Industrial Residuals · Land & Third Places · Historical Dependency · Housing

Then only **finished cases** graduate into the visible Case Studies library.

That distinction is important:

> a planned case is not a case study yet.

### 5. The software portfolio is a founder-capability archive

WeatherSim, Projectr, ToddlerTalk, the Postmortem Lab, and the Software Toolkit are useful because they show that the same engineering instinct appears even where the larger theory is not required. The reference sheet explicitly calls this a **proof layer** for technical judgment, product thinking, public trust, consulting credibility, and funding. 

But I would split it now.

**Promote into current BFL:**

* WeatherSim → Boundary-First Weather
* Postmortem Lab → Boundary-First Engineering
* Software Toolkit → Boundary-First Engineering
* Projectr / Learning Navigator → Corpus Forge / Knowledge Infrastructure where appropriate

**Preserve as selected founder/software work:**

* ToddlerTalk
* older Projectr concepts that do not belong to the current research product

That gives them value without forcing every good thing you've ever designed to become a current BFL product.

### 6. Several “portfolio” files are actually governance

The Artifact Portfolio and Release Lanes is really a **publishing constitution**. Its ORI/ONR/MET/CAS/WIT/FOR/EXP/POS/NAR/GOV/OPS/RES typing and maturity ladder govern how work gets released. Its best rule is already the answer to the current sprawl:

> **One artifact, one primary job.** 

The Monthly Portfolio Review is likewise clearly an **internal operating instrument** for capacity, claims, domain health, burden, and continue/narrow/pause/retire decisions. 

Neither needs to sit inside the public portfolio.

## So I think the website's **Work** architecture should become this

```text
WORK
│
├── Products & Methods
│   ├── Boundary-First Engineering
│   ├── Corpus Forge
│   ├── Agency & AI Governance
│   └── Boundary-First Chess
│
├── Research Programs
│   ├── Boundary-First Weather
│   ├── Boundary-First Soccer
│   ├── RoboCup / Multi-Agent Systems
│   ├── Civilizational Mechanics
│   └── Consequence Accounting
│
├── Case Studies
│   ├── Chess
│   ├── Software / System Failures
│   ├── Representation
│   ├── AI / Agency
│   └── Consequence Accounting
│
├── Selected Software Work
│   ├── Projectr
│   ├── ToddlerTalk
│   └── other founder engineering artifacts
│
└── Work With Us
    ├── Consulting
    ├── Workshops
    ├── Bounded Pilots
    ├── Research Collaboration
    ├── Licensing / Product Partnership
    └── Sponsorship / Funding
```

The nice Boundary-First UX move is that these don't even need to be separate underlying databases. They can be **different projections of one canonical Work graph**.

One object could therefore be:

```text
Corpus Forge
type: program / method / prototype
maturity: operational prototype
public role: product + research infrastructure
available engagement: pilot / consulting
case studies: internal BFL corpus
related programs: Learning Navigator
evidence: provenance preservation / corpus artifacts
```

Then the site can project it differently depending on whether the reader clicks **What We Build**, **Research**, **Case Studies**, or **Work With Us**.

That feels like the important closure here: **the portfolio is not too broad. Its types were insufficiently distinguished.**

Once we type the objects, the breadth becomes an advantage rather than sprawl.
