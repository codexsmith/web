# Public Essay and Talk Architecture
## Working title: People Make the Value

## 1. Core thesis

> **People make the value. People are not overhead.**

Institutions often treat the people who create, use, sustain, and bear the consequence of value as costs outside the value itself. The category error begins in representation and becomes material through budgets, rules, software, and delegated agency.

## 2. Essay architecture

### I. The phrase everyone accepts

Open with `human resources`, `headcount`, `cost center`, or `overhead`. Ask what kind of thing a person becomes inside those terms.

### II. The accountant's defense

Acknowledge the legitimate accounting distinctions. Businesses must represent costs, obligations, and resource flows. The problem is not counting. The problem is forgetting what the count omits.

### III. The category error

Show the asymmetry:

- human output becomes company value;
- sustaining the human becomes company cost;
- transferred damage becomes externality;
- invisible human repair becomes nothing at all.

### IV. The hostage and the accountant

Tell the parable. The rule changes when the hostage becomes someone the accountant loves. The numbers did not change; the boundary of concern moved.

### V. People are the source and destination

Introduce the hierarchy:

> People are the source. People are the destination. Institutions are the means.

Clarify that people are not the only physical input, but value becomes socially meaningful through living beings and their possible futures.

### VI. B2B and agency depth

Acknowledge B2B as delegated service. Then show the failure mode: the chain of delegation becomes so deep that institutions serve institutions and no layer remains answerable to human purpose or consequence.

### VII. The rule must survive proximity

Offer the least-protected-position test. Explain its limitations and role as an empathy and distance diagnostic.

### VIII. Boundary First repair

End with practical questions: who made the value, who receives it, who bears the cost, which capacity is consumed, who can contest, and who repairs?

### IX. Closing

Return to the chant:

```text
People make the value.
People bear the cost.
People are not overhead.
```

## 3. Ten-minute talk

1. One minute - Human resources and overhead.
2. Two minutes - The accounting category versus the person.
3. Two minutes - Hostage and Accountant parable.
4. Two minutes - B2B delegated agency and terminal accountability.
5. Two minutes - Boundary First diagnostic questions.
6. One minute - Call and response.

## 4. Thirty-minute lecture

- 0:00-5:00 - Institutional language and the category error.
- 5:00-10:00 - Externality as a boundary choice.
- 10:00-15:00 - Hostage and Accountant variants.
- 15:00-20:00 - Human capacity, hidden shock absorbers, and false efficiency.
- 20:00-25:00 - B2B agency depth and recursive extraction.
- 25:00-30:00 - Repair instruments and public maxim.

## 5. Workshop prompts

1. Name one value your institution claims.
2. Name the people who actually make and sustain it.
3. Name one human requirement treated as overhead.
4. Name one cost moved outside the reporting boundary.
5. Name the hidden person who repairs the resulting defect.
6. Draw the agency chain from human purpose to final consequence.
7. Identify where the chain stops returning to people.
8. Design one representation, contest, or repair route.

## 6. Audience adaptations

### Workers and public audiences

Lead with the chant, ordinary language, and concrete examples.

### Managers and operators

Lead with capability preservation, hidden repair, turnover, quality, and systemic cost.

### Accountants and finance professionals

Lead with category boundaries, scope, managerial use, externalities, and the danger of ontological overreach. Do not accuse the profession as a whole.

### Software and AI audiences

Lead with executable representations, omitted edge cases, automation of narrow accounts, and responsibility sinks.

### Policy and governance audiences

Lead with affected-party standing, least-protected-position tests, delegated authority, and repair.

### Academic audiences

Separate parable, normative doctrine, accounting claims, organizational theory, and empirical hypotheses.
