# People, Value, and Institutions
## Boundary First Labs Doctrine Seed v0.1

**Class:** Institutional and public doctrine seed  
**Claim ceiling:** Normative and analytical framework; not a complete economic theory

## 1. Core maxim

> **People make the value. People are not overhead.**

## 2. Governing hierarchy

> **People are the source. People are the destination. Institutions are the means.**

This hierarchy does not deny that institutions, tools, ecosystems, capital, and infrastructure contribute to production. It states why organized value has human significance and where institutional agency must ultimately return.

## 3. Constitutive participation

Labor is not merely a claim presented after value has been created. Human attention, skill, judgment, coordination, care, embodiment, memory, trust, maintenance, and repair are constitutive parts of the productive system.

An institution commits a category error when it:

1. claims human output as institutional value;
2. treats the conditions that sustain the human producer as alien overhead;
3. excludes transferred harm from the value calculation;
4. calls the resulting depletion efficiency.

## 4. Cost is not ontology

An accounting category describes a bounded relation inside a specified representation.

A wage may be an operating expense. A payable amount may be a liability. A worker may be assigned to a cost center. None of these descriptions establishes that the human being is fundamentally a cost, liability, or center of burden.

> **The ledger describes the institution's relation to the person. It does not describe the person.**

## 5. The value asymmetry

A defective institution often follows this grammar:

```text
output created by people        -> ours
cost of sustaining people       -> overhead
risk imposed on people          -> externality
repair performed by people      -> invisible
capacity lost by people         -> unmeasured
```

This is not neutral measurement. It is an asymmetrical representation of value and consequence.

## 6. The human-capacity test

Any claimed efficiency should be tested against the capacities it consumes:

- physical and mental health;
- attention and recovery;
- tacit knowledge;
- judgment and discretion;
- trust and cooperative willingness;
- family and community support;
- maintenance and repair labor;
- future option space;
- ecological and infrastructural baselines.

A gain is false or incomplete when it is achieved by depleting enabling capacity outside the reporting boundary.

## 7. People as ends and means

Morally, people should not be reduced to instruments. Operationally, institutions also depend on people as indispensable participants and means of coordinated action.

A defective institution can fail in both directions:

- it does not honor people as ends with standing and rights;
- it forgets that degrading people also destroys the means on which the institution depends.

The institution then consumes its own enabling substrate and calls the consumption productivity.

## 8. Institutional purpose

Institutions are constructed agents. They coordinate capacities that no participant could exercise alone. Their purpose is not self-perpetuation for its own sake. They are justified by the lawful value, capability, stability, and possibility they produce and preserve for people and the systems people inhabit.

## 9. Delegated agency rule

Every institutional delegation should preserve a legible return path:

```text
human purpose
    -> delegated authority
        -> institutional action
            -> represented consequence
                -> human value, standing, or repair
```

Delegation that lacks a terminal return to affected people is structurally open.

## 10. No hidden human shock absorbers

An institution must not appear efficient because people invisibly absorb:

- ambiguity;
- exceptions;
- emotional consequence;
- unsafe defaults;
- overload;
- unowned maintenance;
- record incompatibility;
- customer distress;
- algorithmic error;
- responsibility gaps.

The work must be represented, supported, compensated, redesigned, or stopped.

## 11. Doctrine compression

> People create and sustain the value institutions claim. The costs of human capacity are not alien burdens on the enterprise but conditions of its operation. A business becomes extractive when it captures human output while exporting the cost of sustaining, repairing, or restoring the people and systems that made the output possible.

## 12. Public tests

Before accepting a claim of efficiency, value, or optimization, ask:

1. Who made the value?
2. Who receives the value?
3. Who bears the consequence?
4. Which human requirement is classified as overhead?
5. Which capacity is being consumed without replacement?
6. Which burden crossed the reporting boundary?
7. Who can contest the decision?
8. Who owns repair?
9. Does the agency chain return to people?
10. Does the institution leave its enabling systems more capable or less capable?
