# B2B as Delegated Agency
## Service Extension, Agency Depth, and Recursive Value Extraction

## 1. Initial distinction

Business-to-business activity is not automatically detached from people. Organizations frequently need specialized support to serve their employees, customers, users, patients, citizens, members, or communities.

The constructive form is:

> **B2B is delegated service.**

Institution B extends a capability that institution A needs to perform its human-facing purpose.

## 2. First-order and second-order value engines

### First-order value engine

An organization directly provides a product, service, environment, or capability used by people.

```text
people <-> institution A <-> usable value
```

### Second-order value engine

A supporting organization improves another organization's ability to provide that value.

```text
people <-> institution A <-> institution B
```

Examples can include logistics, payroll, cybersecurity, industrial tooling, accounting, medical equipment, cloud infrastructure, and professional services.

Second-order does not mean inferior. It means one step further from the terminal human use and consequence.

## 3. Agency-depth risk

Each delegation adds distance among:

- the person who authorizes or sustains the system;
- the institution that purchases the service;
- the institution that provides the service;
- the worker who executes the service;
- the person who receives the benefit;
- the person who bears the failure.

Additional depth can improve specialization. It can also weaken consequence visibility, contestability, and repair.

## 4. Pathological closure

The chain becomes pathological when institutional exchange is treated as self-justifying and the human terminus disappears.

```text
business
    -> serves business
        -> serves business
            -> optimizes metric
                -> improves portfolio
                    -> satisfies capital target
```

At that point, value can circulate as institutional claims without a clear account of:

- who gains usable capability;
- whose capacity is consumed;
- who bears the risk;
- what social or ecological system absorbs the cost;
- who can contest the result;
- who must repair the failure.

This is:

> **Recursive agency without terminal accountability.**

## 5. Recursive value extraction

The strongest bounded formulation is not that all B2B is recursive extraction. It is:

> **B2B can become recursive value extraction when every layer optimizes the next institution's accounting while no layer remains answerable to the people and baselines that sustain the chain.**

The extraction pattern is:

1. Human capacity enters the chain.
2. Institutional claims to value move upward.
3. Costs and risks move outward or downward.
4. Decision authority becomes more remote.
5. Responsibility fragments.
6. Human and ecological repair remains local.

## 6. Terminal-accountability test

A B2B service should be able to answer:

- Which people ultimately benefit?
- Which people are affected even if they are not users?
- Which human or environmental capacities does the service consume?
- What does the purchasing business become better able to do?
- What harmful action does it become better able to do?
- Which layer can interrupt the chain?
- Where does responsibility land?
- What evidence reaches affected people?
- How can they contest and reach repair?

## 7. The delegated-service rule

> **Every business serves people directly or by delegation. A business that cannot name its human terminus has not completed its account of purpose or consequence.**

This does not require every employee to know every downstream user. It requires the agency architecture to preserve enough representation to prevent institutional clients, metrics, or balance sheets from becoming the final unquestioned end.

## 8. Design implications

Useful instruments include:

- agency-chain maps;
- value-source and value-destination maps;
- affected-party registers;
- human-capacity ledgers;
- delegated-purpose declarations;
- consequence return paths;
- interruption and appeal mechanisms;
- lifecycle and repair assignments;
- local-global closure tests.

## 9. Compact doctrine

> **B2B is legitimate when it extends human service. It becomes extraction when the agency chain closes before returning value, standing, and repair to people.**
