# Visual and Campaign Concepts
## People Make the Value

## 1. Core campaign identity

**Primary line:** People make the value.  
**Response line:** People are not overhead.  
**Doctrine line:** People are the source. People are the destination. Institutions are the means.

## 2. Visual concept: The Hostage and the Accountant

### Composition

- A person sits in a chair opposite an accountant.
- The hostage's body fades into rows of ledger entries.
- The accountant's wrist is chained to the ledger.
- A second chain runs from the ledger to the hostage.
- The ledger column headings read: cost, liability, risk, externality.
- A faint family photograph sits beside the calculator.

### Interpretive variants

1. The hostage has no face until they speak.
2. The hostage chair is empty and labeled `externality`.
3. The accountant and hostage share the same face.
4. The ledger physically blocks the accountant's view.
5. The final panel reveals the hostage as the accountant's child.


### Poster 2



### Poster 4

```text
PEOPLE ARE THE SOURCE.
PEOPLE ARE THE DESTINATION.

```

Background: a value-flow diagram that begins and ends with people while institutions occupy the middle.

### Poster 5

```text
B2B IS DELEGATED SERVICE.
THE CHAIN MUST RETURN TO PEOPLE.
```

Background: nested company logos or abstract boxes connected to a final human endpoint; broken chains end in metrics.

## 4. Animated explainer

### Duration

60 to 90 seconds.

### Sequence

1. A person performs work; a value token enters a company.
2. The token becomes revenue and rises into the ledger.
3. Wages, care, recovery, and repair move downward as costs.
4. Some costs cross the company boundary and disappear from the screen.
5. The camera follows them into a household, clinic, public budget, and future maintainer.
6. Business boxes delegate to more business boxes.
7. The human endpoint fades.
8. The Boundary First repair redraws the return path.
9. End card: `People make the value. People are not overhead.`

## 5. Interactive web concept

### Title

**Where Did the Value Go?**

### User interaction

The visitor selects a familiar system: retail, hospital, software company, school, delivery platform, public agency, or B2B vendor.

They trace:

- source of value;
- institution that claims value;
- people who receive value;
- people who absorb cost;
- hidden repair labor;
- delegated business layers;
- point where responsibility disappears.

The result produces a `value return map` and a `hidden overhead inversion` score for teaching purposes, not certification.

## 6. Campaign content sequence

1. Slogan card: People make the value.
2. Counter-card: People are not overhead.
3. Parable clip: The hostage speaks with a familiar voice.
4. Explainer: A cost is not a person.
5. B2B card: The chain must return to people.
6. Audit card: Who made it? Who got it? Who paid for it? Who repairs it?
7. Long-form essay or talk.
8. Case study.
9. Practical audit worksheet.

## 7. Tone

- morally direct;
- technically careful;
- anti-dehumanization without anti-accounting caricature;
- critical of structures without erasing individual agency;
- constructive enough to name repair;
- accessible enough to chant;
- exact enough to withstand professional review.
