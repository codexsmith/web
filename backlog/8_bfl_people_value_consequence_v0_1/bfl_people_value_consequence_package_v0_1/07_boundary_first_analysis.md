# Boundary First Analysis
## People, Value, Overhead, and Delegated Institutions

## 1. Declared use

Develop a public and operational framework for diagnosing institutions that claim human-produced value while representing human requirements and consequences as costs to be minimized or exported.

## 2. Consequential boundary

The primary boundary is the institution's authorized account of value and cost.

Inside the boundary may appear:

- revenue;
- assets;
- contractual obligations;
- payroll expense;
- productivity metrics;
- customer accounts;
- investor returns.

Outside or weakly represented may remain:

- embodied depletion;
- care and recovery;
- family and community subsidy;
- tacit knowledge loss;
- emotional labor;
- future repair;
- ecological burden;
- affected noncustomers;
- lost option space.

## 3. Protected invariants

1. People retain standing beyond their classification as worker, customer, dependent, cost, or risk.
2. Value claims remain connected to the capacities and systems that made the value possible.
3. Delegated agency remains answerable to affected people.
4. Responsibility travels with authority and repair capacity.
5. Local savings do not count as complete success when they deplete larger-system capacity.
6. Human requirements are not erased by moving them outside the reporting boundary.

## 4. Root distinctions

- person / accounting category;
- operating expense / ontological burden;
- obligation / liability-bearing human;
- price / value;
- value creation / value capture;
- business client / human terminus;
- local efficiency / systemic capacity;
- delegation / abdication;
- representation / standing;
- distance / absence;
- cost movement / cost resolution;

## 5. Agency map

```text
people
    -> create and authorize institutions
        -> institutions delegate roles and capabilities
            -> workers, managers, software, suppliers, and capital act
                -> consequences cross organizational boundaries
                    -> affected people and systems absorb results
```

The defect appears when the return path from consequence to representation, contest, responsibility, and repair is broken.

## 6. Defect model

### Defect A: human capacity represented only as cost

The institution measures payment and benefits but not the capacity preserved or consumed.

### Defect B: output claimed, reproduction externalized

The institution claims the value created by labor while households, public systems, and workers fund recovery, health, training, and long-term damage.

### Defect C: distance-mediated harm

The decision-maker accepts a rule because the affected person remains abstract or socially remote.

### Defect D: agency recursion

Business layers serve other business layers until institutional performance replaces represented human purpose.

### Defect E: responsibility fog

Every participant follows the books or process, but no actor has both authority and obligation to repair the consequence.

## 7. Failure modes

- headcount reduction treated as value without capability analysis;
- hidden labor supporting an apparently automated system;
- unpaid exception handling;
- benefits captured internally while injury is socialized;
- B2B optimization that strengthens coercive or extractive client behavior;
- policy reversal only after consequence reaches the powerful;
- accounting language used as moral exoneration;
- empathy replacing durable institutional representation;
- slogans promoted without an auditable repair instrument.

## 8. Repair routes

### Consequence accounting

Trace where labor, risk, maintenance, recovery, and environmental burdens land.

### Human-capacity ledger

Represent the capacities the institution consumes, preserves, builds, or destroys.

### Agency-chain declaration

Name the human purpose, each delegated institution, authority transfer, affected scope, and terminal accountability.

### Standing and contestability

Give affected people explanation, evidence access, challenge rights, and a route to correction.

### Responsibility-capacity alignment

Ensure the repair owner has authority, knowledge, time, resources, and obligation.

### Lifecycle stewardship

Represent recruitment, training, operation, recovery, injury, transition, maintenance, and retirement rather than only immediate production.

### Proximity stress test

Test whether a rule remains acceptable when the rule-maker or loved one occupies the least-protected position.

### Local-global closure test

Determine whether the claimed gain preserves the larger systems on which it depends.

## 10. Closure criteria for this project

This doctrine cluster is ready for public pilot when:

- accounting language has been professionally reviewed;
- the public slogan and doctrine are clearly distinguished;
- at least three concrete case studies have been developed;
- the B2B model distinguishes constructive delegation from extraction;
- one audit instrument has been tested on a real organization or process;
- affected-party and labor perspectives have reviewed the framing;
- the package includes correction and supersession paths.
