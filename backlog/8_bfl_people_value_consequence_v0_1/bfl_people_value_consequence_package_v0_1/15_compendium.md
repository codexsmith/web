# Conversation Capture
## The Hostage, the Accountant, and the Human Cost Category Error

**Status:** Source-preserving synthesis  
**Conversation date:** 2026-08-01

## 1. Origin frame

The conversation began with a proposed consequence parable:

> **The Hostage and the Accountant**

The modern accountant is not merely an observer who records consequence. By controlling the authorized account of money, liability, cost, risk, and externality, the accountant can participate in deciding which lives and harms count. The accountant may therefore become both:

- the hostage-taker, because the ledger authorizes or normalizes coercive consequences; and
- the hostage, because the accountant experiences the institution's categories, targets, and approved books as constraints that leave no alternative.

The person holding the ledger can exercise enormous power while sincerely experiencing themselves as powerless.

## 2. Distance-mediated permission to harm

The next development concerned rules made by powerful people for others. A rule may be defended as principled, necessary, moral, efficient, or traditional while its consequences remain socially distant. When the same rule reaches the rule-maker, their child, family, friend, or community, the rule often changes.

The durable indictment is:

> **The rule was principled while it governed strangers. It became negotiable when it reached the author's house.**

The problem is not that proximity can produce empathy. The problem is that another person's humanity was made conditional on proximity to power.

A useful policy test emerged:

> **Would the rule-maker accept the rule unchanged if they or the person they loved most occupied its least-protected position?**

This is not a substitute for law, evidence, or democratic procedure. It is a test for moral anesthesia created by social distance.

## 3. Human resource, asset, liability, and overhead

The conversation then examined the institutional phrase `human resource` and the relationship among assets, liabilities, operating expenses, and people.

The key correction was not simply that an employee is a liability. The sharper category error is that institutions can treat the recurring cost of sustaining human productive capacity as though it were an alien burden on the enterprise.

The business claims the output as value while treating the people and conditions that make the output possible as overhead to be minimized.

The durable statements are:

> **Labor is not a liability attached to the business. Labor is part of what the business is.**

> **The company records the maintenance of human capacity as a burden while recording the output of that capacity as the company's achievement.**

> **The company mistakes the price of having people for the cost of being burdened by them.**

The critique is not that all accounting categories are false. It is that a bounded bookkeeping category can metastasize into an institutional ontology: `labor cost` becomes `the worker is the cost`; `headcount reduction` becomes success; human needs become defects in an otherwise efficient system.

## 4. The slogan breakthrough

The conversation searched for chantable compression. The strongest pair was:

> **People make the value. People are not overhead.**

A fuller hierarchy followed:

> **People are the source. People are the destination. Institutions are the means.**

The phrase `people consume the value` was recognized as too narrow. People create, receive, use, inhabit, preserve, repair, transmit, and transform value. The point is that value has no human meaning without people.

## 5. B2B as delegated agency

The conversation then questioned whether a people-centered account misrepresents business-to-business activity.

The resolution was:

> **B2B is not inherently illegitimate. It is delegated service.**

One institution helps another institution serve people. B2B becomes dangerous when the agency chain loses its human terminus:

```text
people
    -> institution
        -> supporting institution
            -> another institution
                -> metric
                    -> portfolio
                        -> balance sheet
```

At each delegation step, the institutional intermediary becomes the immediate customer while the human source, beneficiary, worker, and consequence-bearer become more remote.

The pathological form is:

> **Recursive agency without terminal accountability.**

Or:

> **The agency chain closes around institutions before value and consequence return to people.**

## 6. Durable conceptual cluster

The conversation produced a coherent cluster of public and research propositions:

1. People make the value.
2. People are not overhead.
3. People are the source and destination of value.
4. Institutions are delegated means, not ultimate ends.
5. Business agency must return to represented human consequence.
6. B2B is legitimate delegated service when it extends human-serving capacity.
7. B2B becomes recursive extraction when institutional value circuits close before returning to people.
8. Distance can anesthetize rule-makers to consequences they would reject at close range.
9. The ledger describes an institutional representation; it does not define the human being.
10. No consequence without representation.

## 7. Provenance note

This package preserves the conceptual development of the conversation. Quotable lines have been lightly normalized for readability. Publication should distinguish direct conversation language, editorial synthesis, technical claims, and later externally validated claims.

# Concept Brief
## People Make the Value

## One-sentence thesis

> **People make the value. People are not overhead.**

## Problem

Modern institutions often claim the benefits produced by human capacity while representing the costs of sustaining that capacity as burdens to be minimized. Labor, care, recovery, accommodation, safety, judgment, maintenance, and repair are treated as overhead even when they are constitutive conditions of the enterprise.

This creates a structural asymmetry:

```text
human output     -> represented as institutional value
human requirement -> represented as institutional cost
human consequence -> often transferred outside the decision boundary
```

## Category error

The error is not the existence of cost accounting. The error occurs when an accounting category is treated as a complete account of the entity represented.

- A wage can be an operating expense.
- An accrued wage can become an accounting liability.
- Neither category establishes that the person is a burden.
- The person is a constitutive participant in the enterprise and a bearer of consequence beyond the ledger.

The ledger describes an obligation or transaction from the institution's viewpoint. It does not define the human being.

## Institutional inversion

The proper hierarchy is:

> **People are the source. People are the destination. Institutions are the means.**

Institutions coordinate people, resources, tools, capital, and knowledge. Their legitimacy and value depend on returning usable value, preserved capacity, and repairable consequence to people and the systems people inhabit.

## B2B extension

Business-to-business activity is second-order delegated agency:

```text
people authorize and sustain institution A
institution A delegates a capability to institution B
institution B should improve institution A's capacity to serve people
```

The model becomes defective when the chain closes around institutional metrics, extraction, or balance-sheet performance without a represented return to human benefit, capacity, standing, or repair.

## Parable

The Hostage and the Accountant dramatizes the same mechanics. The accountant can decide whether the hostage appears as a person, cost, risk, externality, or liability. The accountant may also be trapped by the authorized books. The violence occurs when the representation becomes more authoritative than the embodied consequence.

## Boundary First repair

1. Name the people who create, receive, and bear the value.
2. Map the delegated agency chain.
3. Identify human capacity treated as overhead.
4. Trace burdens crossing the accounting boundary.
5. Restore standing, contestability, responsibility, and repair.
6. Test whether the institution preserves the capacities on which it depends.
7. Prevent local savings from becoming global depletion.

## Public value

This frame gives ordinary people a clearer language for reading institutional claims. It turns terms such as `efficiency`, `headcount`, `liability`, `optimization`, `cost center`, and `externality` into questions:

- What is represented as value?
- Who made it?
- Who receives it?
- Who bears the cost?
- What human capacity is being consumed?
- Which obligation is being renamed a burden?
- Where can the affected person contest and reach repair?

# The Hostage and the Accountant
## A Consequence Parable

## Canonical short form

The accountant sits across from a hostage and calculates the acceptable cost of keeping them alive.

The accountant does not hate the hostage. The accountant has a budget, an authorized category, a target, and a formally defensible procedure.

The hostage appears in the books as a cost, a risk, a liability, or an externality.

Then the hostage turns around.

The accountant recognizes their own child.

The calculation changes immediately.

Nothing about the injury changed. Nothing about the rule changed. Nothing about the hostage's humanity changed.

Only the boundary of concern moved.

## Lesson

> **The rule was acceptable while the hostage was a stranger.**

The parable is not primarily about an evil accountant. It is about a representation that permits human consequence to become administratively remote. The accountant can become both hostage-taker and hostage:

- hostage-taker, because the books authorize decisions that coerce or harm;
- hostage, because the accountant experiences the books as an external necessity.

The ledger distributes agency while obscuring responsibility.

## Variant 1: The accountant across the table

A hostage is bound to a chair. The accountant calmly calculates the cost of keeping the person alive.

**Lesson:** Cost-benefit reasoning becomes violent when the affected person exists only as a number.

## Variant 2: The accountant chained to the ledger

The accountant holds the hostage's chain. A second chain runs from the accountant's wrist into an enormous book.

**Lesson:** The administrator can be both coercive agent and institutional captive. Constraint does not erase agency, but agency cannot be understood without the constraint architecture.

## Variant 3: The same person

The accountant enters figures at a desk. Beneath the desk, every entry tightens a chain around the accountant's own ankle.

**Lesson:** People maintain systems that constrain them because participation is itself a condition of survival.

## Variant 4: The empty chair marked `externality`

The accountant works before an empty hostage chair. The chair is labeled `externality`.

**Lesson:** What is outside the account is not outside consequence. The affected person has disappeared from the decision surface, not from reality.

## Variant 5: The familiar voice

The hostage remains hidden. The accountant approves the cost. Then the hostage speaks with a familiar voice.

**Lesson:** The accountant did not discover a new harm. The accountant discovered a relationship that made the old harm impossible to ignore.

## Variant 6: The corporate recursion

The accountant reports to a manager, the manager to a division, the division to a corporation, the corporation to investors, and every layer says the decision came from another layer.

**Lesson:** Responsibility may be distributed, but it must not be dissolved.

## Policy test

> **Would the rule-maker accept the rule unchanged if they or the person they loved most occupied its least-protected position?**

This proximity test is diagnostic, not sufficient. A fair institution cannot depend on personal familiarity. Its purpose is to expose whether social distance is doing hidden moral work.

## Boundary First reading

- **Boundary:** Who enters the authorized account of consequence?
- **Invariant:** Human standing must not depend on proximity to power.
- **Defect:** Affected people are represented as costs or classes but not as agents.
- **Failure:** The rule appears acceptable only while consequence remains out of sight and earshot.
- **Repair:** Restore affected-party representation, evidence access, contestability, responsibility, and enforceable remedy.

## Closing line

> **The numbers had not changed. Only the boundary of concern had moved.**

# People, Value, and Institutions
## Boundary First Labs Doctrine Seed v0.1

**Class:** Institutional and public doctrine seed  
**Claim ceiling:** Normative and analytical framework; not a complete economic theory

## 1. Core maxim

> **People make the value. People are not overhead.**

## 2. Governing hierarchy

> **People are the source. People are the destination. Institutions are the means.**

This hierarchy does not deny that institutions, tools, ecosystems, capital, and infrastructure contribute to production. It states why organized value has human significance and where institutional agency must ultimately return.

## 3. Constitutive participation

Labor is not merely a claim presented after value has been created. Human attention, skill, judgment, coordination, care, embodiment, memory, trust, maintenance, and repair are constitutive parts of the productive system.

An institution commits a category error when it:

1. claims human output as institutional value;
2. treats the conditions that sustain the human producer as alien overhead;
3. excludes transferred harm from the value calculation;
4. calls the resulting depletion efficiency.

## 4. Cost is not ontology

An accounting category describes a bounded relation inside a specified representation.

A wage may be an operating expense. A payable amount may be a liability. A worker may be assigned to a cost center. None of these descriptions establishes that the human being is fundamentally a cost, liability, or center of burden.

> **The ledger describes the institution's relation to the person. It does not describe the person.**

## 5. The value asymmetry

A defective institution often follows this grammar:

```text
output created by people        -> ours
cost of sustaining people       -> overhead
risk imposed on people          -> externality
repair performed by people      -> invisible
capacity lost by people         -> unmeasured
```

This is not neutral measurement. It is an asymmetrical representation of value and consequence.

## 6. The human-capacity test

Any claimed efficiency should be tested against the capacities it consumes:

- physical and mental health;
- attention and recovery;
- tacit knowledge;
- judgment and discretion;
- trust and cooperative willingness;
- family and community support;
- maintenance and repair labor;
- future option space;
- ecological and infrastructural baselines.

A gain is false or incomplete when it is achieved by depleting enabling capacity outside the reporting boundary.

## 7. People as ends and means

Morally, people should not be reduced to instruments. Operationally, institutions also depend on people as indispensable participants and means of coordinated action.

A defective institution can fail in both directions:

- it does not honor people as ends with standing and rights;
- it forgets that degrading people also destroys the means on which the institution depends.

The institution then consumes its own enabling substrate and calls the consumption productivity.

## 8. Institutional purpose

Institutions are constructed agents. They coordinate capacities that no participant could exercise alone. Their purpose is not self-perpetuation for its own sake. They are justified by the lawful value, capability, stability, and possibility they produce and preserve for people and the systems people inhabit.

## 9. Delegated agency rule

Every institutional delegation should preserve a legible return path:

```text
human purpose
    -> delegated authority
        -> institutional action
            -> represented consequence
                -> human value, standing, or repair
```

Delegation that lacks a terminal return to affected people is structurally open.

## 10. No hidden human shock absorbers

An institution must not appear efficient because people invisibly absorb:

- ambiguity;
- exceptions;
- emotional consequence;
- unsafe defaults;
- overload;
- unowned maintenance;
- record incompatibility;
- customer distress;
- algorithmic error;
- responsibility gaps.

The work must be represented, supported, compensated, redesigned, or stopped.

## 11. Doctrine compression

> People create and sustain the value institutions claim. The costs of human capacity are not alien burdens on the enterprise but conditions of its operation. A business becomes extractive when it captures human output while exporting the cost of sustaining, repairing, or restoring the people and systems that made the output possible.

## 12. Public tests

Before accepting a claim of efficiency, value, or optimization, ask:

1. Who made the value?
2. Who receives the value?
3. Who bears the consequence?
4. Which human requirement is classified as overhead?
5. Which capacity is being consumed without replacement?
6. Which burden crossed the reporting boundary?
7. Who can contest the decision?
8. Who owns repair?
9. Does the agency chain return to people?
10. Does the institution leave its enabling systems more capable or less capable?

# B2B as Delegated Agency
## Service Extension, Agency Depth, and Recursive Value Extraction

## 1. Initial distinction

Business-to-business activity is not automatically detached from people. Organizations frequently need specialized support to serve their employees, customers, users, patients, citizens, members, or communities.

The constructive form is:

> **B2B is delegated service.**

Institution B extends a capability that institution A needs to perform its human-facing purpose.

## 2. First-order and second-order value engines

### First-order value engine

An organization directly provides a product, service, environment, or capability used by people.

```text
people <-> institution A <-> usable value
```

### Second-order value engine

A supporting organization improves another organization's ability to provide that value.

```text
people <-> institution A <-> institution B
```

Examples can include logistics, payroll, cybersecurity, industrial tooling, accounting, medical equipment, cloud infrastructure, and professional services.

Second-order does not mean inferior. It means one step further from the terminal human use and consequence.

## 3. Agency-depth risk

Each delegation adds distance among:

- the person who authorizes or sustains the system;
- the institution that purchases the service;
- the institution that provides the service;
- the worker who executes the service;
- the person who receives the benefit;
- the person who bears the failure.

Additional depth can improve specialization. It can also weaken consequence visibility, contestability, and repair.

## 4. Pathological closure

The chain becomes pathological when institutional exchange is treated as self-justifying and the human terminus disappears.

```text
business
    -> serves business
        -> serves business
            -> optimizes metric
                -> improves portfolio
                    -> satisfies capital target
```

At that point, value can circulate as institutional claims without a clear account of:

- who gains usable capability;
- whose capacity is consumed;
- who bears the risk;
- what social or ecological system absorbs the cost;
- who can contest the result;
- who must repair the failure.

This is:

> **Recursive agency without terminal accountability.**

## 5. Recursive value extraction

The strongest bounded formulation is not that all B2B is recursive extraction. It is:

> **B2B can become recursive value extraction when every layer optimizes the next institution's accounting while no layer remains answerable to the people and baselines that sustain the chain.**

The extraction pattern is:

1. Human capacity enters the chain.
2. Institutional claims to value move upward.
3. Costs and risks move outward or downward.
4. Decision authority becomes more remote.
5. Responsibility fragments.
6. Human and ecological repair remains local.

## 6. Terminal-accountability test

A B2B service should be able to answer:

- Which people ultimately benefit?
- Which people are affected even if they are not users?
- Which human or environmental capacities does the service consume?
- What does the purchasing business become better able to do?
- What harmful action does it become better able to do?
- Which layer can interrupt the chain?
- Where does responsibility land?
- What evidence reaches affected people?
- How can they contest and reach repair?

## 7. The delegated-service rule

> **Every business serves people directly or by delegation. A business that cannot name its human terminus has not completed its account of purpose or consequence.**

This does not require every employee to know every downstream user. It requires the agency architecture to preserve enough representation to prevent institutional clients, metrics, or balance sheets from becoming the final unquestioned end.

## 8. Design implications

Useful instruments include:

- agency-chain maps;
- value-source and value-destination maps;
- affected-party registers;
- human-capacity ledgers;
- delegated-purpose declarations;
- consequence return paths;
- interruption and appeal mechanisms;
- lifecycle and repair assignments;
- local-global closure tests.

## 9. Compact doctrine

> **B2B is legitimate when it extends human service. It becomes extraction when the agency chain closes before returning value, standing, and repair to people.**

# Boundary First Analysis
## People, Value, Overhead, and Delegated Institutions

## 1. Declared use

Develop a public and operational framework for diagnosing institutions that claim human-produced value while representing human requirements and consequences as costs to be minimized or exported.

## 2. Consequential boundary

The primary boundary is the institution's authorized account of value and cost.

Inside the boundary may appear:

- revenue;
- assets;
- contractual obligations;
- payroll expense;
- productivity metrics;
- customer accounts;
- investor returns.

Outside or weakly represented may remain:

- embodied depletion;
- care and recovery;
- family and community subsidy;
- tacit knowledge loss;
- emotional labor;
- future repair;
- ecological burden;
- affected noncustomers;
- lost option space.

## 3. Protected invariants

1. People retain standing beyond their classification as worker, customer, dependent, cost, or risk.
2. Value claims remain connected to the capacities and systems that made the value possible.
3. Delegated agency remains answerable to affected people.
4. Responsibility travels with authority and repair capacity.
5. Local savings do not count as complete success when they deplete larger-system capacity.
6. Human requirements are not erased by moving them outside the reporting boundary.

## 4. Root distinctions

- person / accounting category;
- operating expense / ontological burden;
- obligation / liability-bearing human;
- price / value;
- value creation / value capture;
- business client / human terminus;
- local efficiency / systemic capacity;
- delegation / abdication;
- representation / standing;
- distance / absence;
- cost movement / cost resolution;

## 5. Agency map

```text
people
    -> create and authorize institutions
        -> institutions delegate roles and capabilities
            -> workers, managers, software, suppliers, and capital act
                -> consequences cross organizational boundaries
                    -> affected people and systems absorb results
```

The defect appears when the return path from consequence to representation, contest, responsibility, and repair is broken.

## 6. Defect model

### Defect A: human capacity represented only as cost

The institution measures payment and benefits but not the capacity preserved or consumed.

### Defect B: output claimed, reproduction externalized

The institution claims the value created by labor while households, public systems, and workers fund recovery, health, training, and long-term damage.

### Defect C: distance-mediated harm

The decision-maker accepts a rule because the affected person remains abstract or socially remote.

### Defect D: agency recursion

Business layers serve other business layers until institutional performance replaces represented human purpose.

### Defect E: responsibility fog

Every participant follows the books or process, but no actor has both authority and obligation to repair the consequence.

## 7. Failure modes

- headcount reduction treated as value without capability analysis;
- hidden labor supporting an apparently automated system;
- unpaid exception handling;
- benefits captured internally while injury is socialized;
- B2B optimization that strengthens coercive or extractive client behavior;
- policy reversal only after consequence reaches the powerful;
- accounting language used as moral exoneration;
- empathy replacing durable institutional representation;
- slogans promoted without an auditable repair instrument.

## 8. Repair routes

### Consequence accounting

Trace where labor, risk, maintenance, recovery, and environmental burdens land.

### Human-capacity ledger

Represent the capacities the institution consumes, preserves, builds, or destroys.

### Agency-chain declaration

Name the human purpose, each delegated institution, authority transfer, affected scope, and terminal accountability.

### Standing and contestability

Give affected people explanation, evidence access, challenge rights, and a route to correction.

### Responsibility-capacity alignment

Ensure the repair owner has authority, knowledge, time, resources, and obligation.

### Lifecycle stewardship

Represent recruitment, training, operation, recovery, injury, transition, maintenance, and retirement rather than only immediate production.

### Proximity stress test

Test whether a rule remains acceptable when the rule-maker or loved one occupies the least-protected position.

### Local-global closure test

Determine whether the claimed gain preserves the larger systems on which it depends.

## 9. Candidate instruments

1. **People Are Not Overhead Audit**
2. **Human Capacity and Hidden Cost Ledger**
3. **Delegated Agency Return Map**
4. **B2B Terminal Accountability Test**
5. **Rule Proximity Stress Test**
6. **Value Source-Destination Map**
7. **Hidden Human Shock Absorber Register**
8. **Local Savings / Global Depletion Review**

## 10. Closure criteria for this project

## 3. Accounting distinction

The project should preserve the following distinctions:

- **Expense:** a cost recognized in producing revenue or operating the organization during a period.
- **Liability:** a present obligation arising from past events that may require future transfer of resources.
- **Accrued wages or payroll payable:** compensation earned but not yet paid may be recorded as a liability.
- **Employee or human being:** not reducible to the liability or expense category used to describe a transaction or obligation.
- **Overhead:** an allocation category for indirect costs; use varies by organization and accounting system.

These descriptions should be verified against authoritative accounting sources before formal publication.

## 4. Strong claim boundary

The doctrine does not claim:

- that wages are never expenses;
- that unpaid compensation can never be an accounting liability;
- that all overhead classification is abusive;
- that all B2B models are extractive;
- that all institutions consciously dehumanize people;
- that every efficiency gain hides harm;
- that only human labor produces value;
- that proximity always produces just policy;
- that accounting should represent every human value monetarily;
- that business institutions have no legitimate continuity interests;
- that people are merely means of production.

## 5. Intended claim

The intended claim is narrower and stronger:

> A bounded accounting representation becomes institutionally dangerous when its categories are treated as a complete ontology, when the institution claims human-produced output while classifying the conditions of human capacity as alien burdens, or when costs and consequences are moved beyond the reporting boundary and then treated as resolved.

## 6. Required validation

Before a formal whitepaper or policy instrument:

1. Review financial-accounting terminology with a CPA or accounting scholar.
2. Compare managerial accounting, financial accounting, human-capital accounting, and cost accounting.
3. Review labor economics and theories of the firm.
4. Review stakeholder, cooperative, public-benefit, and fiduciary models.
5. Test the B2B agency model against constructive and harmful cases.
6. Interview workers who perform hidden repair and exception labor.
7. Review disability, care, labor, and human-factors scholarship.
8. Review relevant employment and corporate law with counsel before legal claims.

## 7. Language guidance

### Prefer

- `A wage is an operating cost; a person is not overhead.`
- `The category describes the transaction, not the human being.`
- `People make and sustain the value institutions claim.`
- `B2B is delegated service when the chain returns to people.`
- `The institution captured the gain while exporting the repair burden.`

### Avoid without qualification

- `Employees are assets.`
- `Employees are liabilities.`
- `Labor is never a liability.`
- `B2B is inherently parasitic.`
- `All profit is theft.`
- `Every institution exists only for customers.`
- `People are only the means of production.`

## 8. Publication note

The slogan may be emotionally forceful. The surrounding doctrine must be more exact than the slogan. Boundary First Labs should use the slogan to open inquiry, not to close it.

# Public Essay and Talk Architecture
## Working title: People Make the Value

## 1. Core thesis

> **People make the value. People are not overhead.**

Institutions often treat the people who create, use, sustain, and bear the consequence of value as costs outside the value itself. The category error begins in representation and becomes material through budgets, rules, software, and delegated agency.

## 2. Essay architecture

### I. The phrase everyone accepts

Open with `human resources`, `headcount`, `cost center`, or `overhead`. Ask what kind of thing a person becomes inside those terms.

### II. The accountant's defense

Acknowledge the legitimate accounting distinctions. Businesses must represent costs, obligations, and resource flows. The problem is not counting. The problem is forgetting what the count omits.

### III. The category error

Show the asymmetry:

- human output becomes company value;
- sustaining the human becomes company cost;
- transferred damage becomes externality;
- invisible human repair becomes nothing at all.

### IV. The hostage and the accountant

Tell the parable. The rule changes when the hostage becomes someone the accountant loves. The numbers did not change; the boundary of concern moved.

### V. People are the source and destination

Introduce the hierarchy:

> People are the source. People are the destination. Institutions are the means.

Clarify that people are not the only physical input, but value becomes socially meaningful through living beings and their possible futures.

### VI. B2B and agency depth

Acknowledge B2B as delegated service. Then show the failure mode: the chain of delegation becomes so deep that institutions serve institutions and no layer remains answerable to human purpose or consequence.

### VII. The rule must survive proximity

Offer the least-protected-position test. Explain its limitations and role as an empathy and distance diagnostic.

### VIII. Boundary First repair

End with practical questions: who made the value, who receives it, who bears the cost, which capacity is consumed, who can contest, and who repairs?

### IX. Closing

Return to the chant:

```text
People make the value.
People bear the cost.
People are not overhead.
```

## 3. Ten-minute talk

1. One minute - Human resources and overhead.
2. Two minutes - The accounting category versus the person.
3. Two minutes - Hostage and Accountant parable.
4. Two minutes - B2B delegated agency and terminal accountability.
5. Two minutes - Boundary First diagnostic questions.
6. One minute - Call and response.

## 4. Thirty-minute lecture

- 0:00-5:00 - Institutional language and the category error.
- 5:00-10:00 - Externality as a boundary choice.
- 10:00-15:00 - Hostage and Accountant variants.
- 15:00-20:00 - Human capacity, hidden shock absorbers, and false efficiency.
- 20:00-25:00 - B2B agency depth and recursive extraction.
- 25:00-30:00 - Repair instruments and public maxim.

## 5. Workshop prompts

1. Name one value your institution claims.
2. Name the people who actually make and sustain it.
3. Name one human requirement treated as overhead.
4. Name one cost moved outside the reporting boundary.
5. Name the hidden person who repairs the resulting defect.
6. Draw the agency chain from human purpose to final consequence.
7. Identify where the chain stops returning to people.
8. Design one representation, contest, or repair route.

## 6. Audience adaptations

### Workers and public audiences

Lead with the chant, ordinary language, and concrete examples.

### Managers and operators

Lead with capability preservation, hidden repair, turnover, quality, and systemic cost.

### Accountants and finance professionals

Lead with category boundaries, scope, managerial use, externalities, and the danger of ontological overreach. Do not accuse the profession as a whole.

### Software and AI audiences

Lead with executable representations, omitted edge cases, automation of narrow accounts, and responsibility sinks.

### Policy and governance audiences

Lead with affected-party standing, least-protected-position tests, delegated authority, and repair.

### Academic audiences

Separate parable, normative doctrine, accounting claims, organizational theory, and empirical hypotheses.
