# Product Owner — Public Copy Reframe: Familiar Machinery Before Formalism

**Status:** active product-owner direction / copy requirements  
**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Owner workstream:** `20_public_front_door_and_projection_consolidation`  
**Scope:** homepage orientation, Method, Research, About, Distinction Space, Schemathematics, public trust language, and the order in which visitors encounter the formal stack  
**Implementation posture:** backlog authority only; this document does not directly mutate live site copy

## 1. Product-owner decision

The public website should change the **order in which a visitor is asked to believe things**.

Boundary First Labs should not introduce itself by asking a cold visitor to accept Boundary Theory, Distinction Space, Schemathematics, or any broad foundational claim before the visitor understands the familiar machinery underneath the work.

The public sequence should instead be:

```text
familiar machinery
    -> recurring coordination problem
        -> Boundary First method
            -> Boundary First Labs as institution
                -> research question
                    -> Boundary Theory
                        -> Distinction Space / Representational Mechanics / Schemathematics
                            -> evidence, experiments, formal results, failures, and applications
```

The site should make the deepest theory easier to understand by beginning from concepts the visitor already recognizes:

- state;
- transition;
- boundary;
- constraint;
- interface;
- invariant;
- observation / evidence;
- failure / defect;
- repair;
- closure.

These are **not presented as inventions of Boundary First Labs**.

The public promise is that BFL makes recurring machinery explicit, comparable, testable, composable, and, where useful, executable.

## 2. Governing positioning

The preferred institutional framing is:

> **Complex systems already run on familiar machinery.**  
> States. Transitions. Boundaries. Constraints. Interfaces. Invariants. Evidence. Failure. Repair.  
>  
> **Boundary First Labs studies how that machinery fits together—and builds ways to make it explicit, testable, and usable.**

The institutional provenance line remains:

> **Practice-born. Research-backed. Formally generalized.**

The short campaign line should prefer:

> **Familiar machinery. Made explicit.**

A more conversational variant may be used where appropriate:

> **The machinery was already there. We make it visible.**

Avoid making **“Old machinery”** the primary public line. The intended meaning is inherited/familiar, not obsolete.

## 3. Why this is the correct public sequence

The current site contains a serious and increasingly disciplined formal research program. The problem is not lack of depth. The problem is the **entry cost** imposed when the visitor encounters the deepest vocabulary before the practical and historical reason for that vocabulary is legible.

A cold visitor should not need to decide whether BFL has invented a new foundation before they can understand what the Lab does.

The website should first establish a recognizable observation:

> Mature systems already rely on state, change, constraints, interfaces, evidence, and repair.

Then establish the coordination problem:

> Those roles are often distributed across different models, procedures, tools, disciplines, and representations.

Then introduce the method:

> Boundary First makes consequential structure explicit before optimization, compression, or implementation causes it to disappear.

Only after that does the site introduce the formal research question:

> How much of this recurring machinery can be coordinated, compared, transported, or formalized without erasing the mature theories that already govern the underlying domains?

This ordering lowers the belief burden while raising the rigor burden.

The visitor is not asked to accept a new ontology. The Lab must earn each stronger claim through evidence, comparison, execution, theorem, counterexample, benchmark, or other bounded result.

## 4. Copy invariants

All public copy produced under this reframe should preserve the following invariants.

### 4.1 Familiar machinery is inherited machinery

Do not imply that BFL invented state machines, state spaces, transition systems, control, invariants, interfaces, formal methods, dynamical systems, provenance, maintainability, systems engineering, category-theoretic composition, or scientific measurement.

### 4.2 Systematization is a contribution class, not proof of novelty

The site may claim that BFL is attempting to coordinate, systematize, instrument, compare, or execute recurring structure.

It may not silently convert recurrence into proof of a new universal foundation.

### 4.3 Native formalisms remain authoritative in their domains

Where existing mathematics, engineering, scientific practice, or formal methods already carry the required structure, the public copy should say so.

A BFL representation is useful only if it adds a bounded consequence: clearer dependency, better translation, defect exposure, reconstructibility, executable comparison, pedagogy, measured practical value, theorem, counterexample, or another explicit result.

### 4.4 Analogy is not equivalence

The same analytical slot may recur in software, physics, mathematics, research operations, and institutions without those domains being mathematically identical.

### 4.5 Public orientation should not require ontology

A visitor should be able to understand what BFL does before choosing whether to inspect the deeper formal program.

### 4.6 Formal claims must remain local

When the Lab believes it has something genuinely new, state the smallest defensible novelty unit:

- definition;
- relation;
- theorem;
- counterexample;
- classification;
- executable construction;
- diagnostic distinction;
- measured advantage;
- or integration that produces a result unavailable independently.

Do not make the entire theoretical stack carry the novelty claim.

## 5. Homepage copy direction

### 5.1 Hero / first conceptual statement

Preferred default:

> **Complex systems already run on familiar machinery.**
>
> States. Transitions. Boundaries. Constraints. Interfaces. Invariants. Evidence. Failure. Repair.
>
> **Boundary First Labs studies how that machinery fits together—and builds ways to make it explicit, testable, and usable.**

Secondary institutional line:

> **Practice-born. Research-backed. Formally generalized.**

The homepage does not need to enumerate the formal stack in the hero.

### 5.2 Immediate follow-up: why BFL exists

The next copy block should answer the obvious question: if this machinery already exists, why does BFL need to exist?

Preferred direction:

> Every mature field already has ways to reason about state, change, constraints, boundaries, evidence, and failure.
>
> But those ideas are often distributed across different theories, tools, diagrams, procedures, and professional practices.
>
> **Boundary First asks what happens when those roles are made explicit together—without discarding the specialized machinery that already works.**

Then connect the payoff:

> We use that common structure to compare representations, trace transformations, expose hidden assumptions, diagnose defects, preserve consequential information, and design paths to repair.

### 5.3 Homepage visual / instrument

Introduce a simple state-transition machine before the deepest theory stack.

Conceptual content:

```text
                TRANSFORMATION
STATE  ------------------------------>  STATE'
  |                                        |
  |                                        |
  +-- boundary                             +-- consequence
  +-- constraints                          +-- evidence
  +-- admissibility                        +-- defect
  +-- invariants                           +-- repair
  +-- interfaces                           +-- closure
```

Preferred caption:

> **The state transition isn't the whole story.**

Supporting questions:

- What makes the state valid?
- Which changes are allowed?
- What has to survive the change?
- How do we know it occurred?
- Who or what crosses the boundary?
- What happens when an assumption fails?
- What does repair actually restore?

This object should function as a recognizable public on-ramp, not as proof that all systems share one formal state model.

## 6. Homepage narrative sequence

The public homepage should tell one coherent story before the visitor enters the Lab Machine.

```text
1. FAMILIAR MACHINERY
   State. Boundary. Transformation. Constraint. Interface. Evidence. Failure. Repair.

2. THE COORDINATION PROBLEM
   The machinery exists, but is often distributed across representations, tools, disciplines, and procedures.

3. BOUNDARY FIRST
   Make consequential structure explicit.

4. THE LAB
   Build methods, software, instruments, research, and public artifacts around that practice.

5. THE RESEARCH QUESTION
   How much of the recurring structure actually generalizes?

6. THE FORMAL PROGRAM
   Boundary Theory -> Distinction Space -> Representational Mechanics -> Schemathematics.

7. EVIDENCE
   Products. Experiments. Publications. Case studies. Formal results. Failed hypotheses. Counterexamples.

8. INVITATION
   Use it. Test it. Criticize it. Bring a problem. Collaborate. Enter the Lab.
```

This sequence composes with the existing Pass-20 front-door architecture. It does not replace the flagship work, Timeline, Paper Mine, Research Lane, sandbox, intake, or **Enter the Lab** decisions. It supplies the copy logic that connects them.

## 7. Method page copy requirements

The Method page is already structurally strong. The existing kernel—Orient, Distinguish, Bound, Represent, Traverse, Transform, Witness, Compare, Detect Defect, Repair, Close/Recurse—should be preserved.

The change is the introduction.

Preferred opening:

> **Boundary First begins with an old engineering move: represent the system, its state, and the transformations available to it.**
>
> It extends that move by requiring the consequential structure around the representation to remain visible: boundaries, distinctions, constraints, interfaces, invariants, evidence, consequences, defects, and repair.

Preferred method firewall:

> **Boundary First is not a demand that every domain use the same mathematics. It is a demand that consequential assumptions and transformations stop disappearing merely because we changed representations.**

This makes the existing method kernel read as instrumented reasoning rather than proprietary philosophical vocabulary.

The live `src/content/lab-machine-method.json` already contains strong supporting language and should be revised conservatively rather than rewritten wholesale.

Preserve in particular:

- domain-native inputs;
- explicit failure modes;
- the distinction between representation and execution;
- witness/critique/repair loops;
- the rule that formalism has failed if it merely renames established structure.

## 8. Research page copy requirements

The Research page should lead with a **question**, not a theory hierarchy.

Preferred headline:

> **What recurring machinery is already here?**

Preferred introduction:

> Across computing, mathematics, physics, control, engineering, formal methods, and scientific practice, similar analytical roles appear repeatedly: state, distinction, boundary, transformation, admissibility, invariant, observation, closure, and defect.
>
> **Boundary First Labs studies how far those similarities actually go.**

Immediately establish negative capability:

> Sometimes the answer may be: not very far.
>
> Similar vocabulary does not establish mathematical equivalence. A useful analogy is not a theorem. A shared representation is not proof of a shared ontology.

Then state the actual research program:

> The research program asks which structures genuinely transport across domains, which remain domain-specific, which reduce to established theory, and which—if any—produce new formal consequences when coordinated.

### 8.1 Boundary Theory public definition

Replace umbrella/foundation-first language with:

> **Boundary Theory is BFL's research program for testing whether recurring structures of state, distinction, boundary, transformation, admissibility, and closure can be coordinated without erasing the mature theories that already govern their domains.**

The live `src/content/lab-machine-research.json` already has strong claim boundaries, counterexample search, matched comparison, and negative-capable stages. Preserve those. The primary change is the public role/description and entry ordering.

## 9. Distinction Space public copy requirements

Distinction Space should become easier to understand by presenting it as an explicit analytical profile rather than as a successor to state-space mathematics.

Preferred introduction:

> **A Distinction Space is BFL's attempt to make the full analytical profile of a represented system explicit.**

Then:

> A conventional state description may tell us which configurations are possible. For some problems, that is enough.
>
> Other problems also require us to track which differences matter, which transformations are lawful, what must remain invariant, how the system is observed, what crosses its interfaces, what information is forgotten, what constitutes failure, and what repair means.
>
> **Distinction Space is a formal research object for representing those requirements together.**

Required firewall:

> **It is not intended to replace a domain's native mathematics when that mathematics already carries the required structure.**

The natural visitor question should become:

> When does the additional explicit structure actually buy us something?

That is a desirable research question, not a weakness in the page.

Public copy should make the answer evidence-driven: reconstruction, comparison, translation, defect detection, execution, theorem/counterexample, or measured utility.

## 10. Schemathematics public copy requirements

Schemathematics should not be introduced as “another kind of mathematics.”

Preferred framing:

> **Mathematics is full of objects that look different while participating in similar operations: decomposition, transformation, quotienting, recursion, closure, projection, and composition.**
>
> Schemathematics asks whether those operative structures can be represented explicitly enough to compare, transform, navigate, and sometimes execute.

Required authority rule:

> **The native mathematics remains authoritative. The schema is a working representation of how the object behaves.**

This framing should remain compatible with the existing Schemathematics claim boundary in `src/content/lab-machine-research.json` and the dedicated `src/content/product-landing-pages/schemathematics.json` material.

The page should explicitly allow the outcome that the schema adds no useful value for a given object or task.

## 11. About page copy requirements

The About page should tell the institutional story in the same direction as the theory reframe.

Preferred opening narrative:

> **Boundary First Labs grew out of applied software and systems work, scientific practice, and a long-running effort to understand why the same classes of problems kept appearing in very different places.**
>
> Interfaces failed. Representations hid consequential information. Local decisions produced global defects. State changed without adequate provenance. Repairs remained invisible. Abstractions leaked.
>
> **Over time, those recurring problems became a method. The method became a research program. The research program became formal machinery.**

Then:

> **Practice-born. Research-backed. Formally generalized.**

The site should communicate the causal direction as:

```text
scientific inquiry
+ software / systems practice
+ delivery / consulting / public systems work
        -> recurring structural problems
        -> explicit method
        -> comparative research
        -> formalization
        -> Boundary First Labs
```

Not:

```text
new theory
        -> applications selected afterward
```

The existing `src/content/lab-machine-about.json` already contains the right evidence distinctions and the important principle **Prefer examples before ontology**. Preserve those controls while updating the provenance compression and public introduction.

## 12. First-class trust component: Built from established machinery

Add a visible public trust/legibility component rather than leaving this material buried in disclaimers.

Suggested heading:

> **Built from established machinery**

Preferred copy:

> BFL did not invent state machines, state spaces, transition systems, control theory, invariants, interfaces, formal methods, dynamical systems, provenance, category-theoretic composition, systems engineering, or software maintainability.
>
> **We study recurring structure across these traditions, build explicit representations of it, and test whether coordinating that structure produces useful methods, instruments, software, or formal results.**

This section is not defensive copy. It establishes epistemic competence.

It should increase the credibility of later novelty claims because the visitor can see that BFL distinguishes inheritance from contribution.

## 13. Tone requirements

The site should sound:

- technically serious;
- curious rather than prophetic;
- confident about the existence of the work;
- careful about what the work proves;
- accessible without pretending the underlying material is simple;
- willing to state negative or reduction outcomes;
- comfortable saying “existing machinery is sufficient here.”

Avoid:

- grandiosity as a substitute for mechanism;
- “new foundations” as homepage shorthand;
- “more fundamental than” without a bounded formal result;
- broad claims of universal reduction;
- framing mature adjacent disciplines as incomplete merely because BFL uses a larger vocabulary;
- copy that makes visitors learn the internal taxonomy before understanding the practical problem.

## 14. Copy hierarchy / belief burden

The site should distinguish the following claim levels in both wording and information architecture.

### Level 0 — observable practice

Systems have states, transitions, boundaries, constraints, interfaces, evidence, failure, and repair.

### Level 1 — recurring analytical pattern

Comparable roles recur across multiple domains.

### Level 2 — BFL systematization

BFL coordinates those roles into explicit methods and representations.

### Level 3 — usefulness claim

The coordination improves a bounded task: understanding, comparison, reconstruction, diagnosis, execution, pedagogy, or delivery.

### Level 4 — formal research result

A theorem, counterexample, classification, typed relation, benchmark, or other bounded formal result is established.

### Level 5 — broader generalization

A cross-domain or foundational conclusion is justified only to the degree supported by the accumulated formal and empirical evidence.

The website should never visually or rhetorically jump from Level 1 directly to Level 5.

## 15. Relationship to flagship work

The reframe should strengthen, not displace, the Pass-20 decision to explain the Lab through concrete work.

Software Before Code becomes an especially important bridge because it demonstrates the causal story:


Boundary First Chess, Weather at Home, sandboxes, public infrastructure work, and other bounded applications should be allowed to demonstrate the method without being presented as proofs of the entire theory.

## 19. Source authority

The copy direction is downstream of the current Boundary First Labs theoretical normalization completed in the Lab repository.

Relevant current authorities include the theoretical-core systematization doctrine, theoretical architecture, primitive/dependency doctrine, Distinction Space definition, boundary analytic-role doctrine, claims/falsification controls, and terminology/supersession map.

The web repository should **project** those claim boundaries rather than becoming a second independent source of theory truth.

If the Lab theory terminology changes again before implementation, resolve against the current Lab authority rather than freezing this backlog artifact as mathematical canon.

The product-owner decisions that should survive reasonable terminology updates are:

1. familiar machinery before proprietary/formal taxonomy;
2. examples before ontology;
3. inherited machinery is named as inherited;
4. systematization is separated from novelty;
5. native formalisms remain authoritative where sufficient;
6. the research program remains negative-capable;
7. formal novelty is local and earned;
8. the public front door reduces belief burden without flattening research depth.

## 20. Non-goals

This copy reframe does **not** mean:

- hiding Boundary Theory;
- weakening serious formal research;
- removing Distinction Space or Schemathematics;
- replacing technical pages with generic marketing copy;
- pretending BFL is only a software consultancy;
- denying potentially novel results where evidence exists;
- reducing all domains to one state-machine formalism;
- replacing the Lab Machine;
- removing the apparatus / machine visual grammar;
- deleting historical provenance that used stronger language.

The objective is to make the strong work easier to evaluate by stating the inherited machinery and claim boundaries first.

## 21. Acceptance criteria

This product-owner requirement is satisfied when a cold visitor can reach the following conclusions from the public site without first accepting any novel foundational claim:

- Boundary First Labs is an applied research and product laboratory.
- It grew from practice and research rather than from a vocabulary-first theory exercise.
- It works with familiar system machinery such as states, transformations, boundaries, constraints, interfaces, evidence, and repair.
- BFL is attempting to make recurring analytical roles explicit and coordinated.
- Boundary First is a method for preserving consequential structure through representation and transformation.
- Boundary Theory is the research program testing how far that coordination genuinely goes.
- Distinction Space is a candidate explicit analytical profile, not an automatic replacement for conventional state spaces.
- Schemathematics is a representational/formal research program over existing mathematics, not authority over the native mathematics.
- Similarity across domains is a clue, not proof of equivalence.
- BFL accepts reduction, counterexample, and “existing machinery is sufficient” as legitimate research outcomes.
- Strong novelty claims, when made, can be located at a specific formal or empirical result.
- The visitor can see concrete work before deciding whether to enter the deeper research machine.

## 22. Product-owner summary

The governing sentence for this website pass is:

> **BFL does not begin by asking you to believe in a new theory. It begins by showing you machinery you already recognize—and then asks what we can learn by making it explicit.**

The public website should earn curiosity first, understanding second, and belief only where evidence warrants it.
