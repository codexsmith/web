## Turn 16 — Assistant

The pointer problem was framed as a projection defect. Perspective projection maps many world points to one screen coordinate, while the ordinary cursor behaves as though the inverse were unique.

The cursor was reframed as a spatial interaction field rather than a point:

```text
I_t = (view ray, depth interval, candidate objects,
       task context, action mode, interaction history)
```

Selection became progressive closure:

```text
orient → expose candidates → constrain depth and intent
→ preview target → commit
```

Four states were defined:

- Orient;
- Probe;
- Bind;
- Act.

The mouse could remain usable by mapping motion to orientation, the wheel to depth, binding to local frames, and modifiers to semantic scale. Depth was extended beyond geometry to containment, evidence, version, dependency, disclosure, and maturity.

The binding protocol was expanded to describe pointer model, candidate resolution, depth control, selection hysteresis, preview, local-frame binding, admissibility, and reasons for blocked actions.

The first recommended prototype was deliberately small and measurable, comparing conventional ray casting, menu disambiguation, and a boundary-aware spatial pointer.
