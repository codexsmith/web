# Research and Validation Plan

## 1. Research claims under test

### H1 — Institutional entrance

Role- and artifact-recognizable entrances improve comprehension of Boundary First Labs relative to a theory-first landing page.

### H2 — Atlas progressive disclosure

Cluster-first overview plus guided camera paths improves orientation and recall relative to an edge-dense graph presented immediately.

### H3 — Claim-status visibility

Persistent status, claim ceiling, and provenance reduce overinterpretation of work-in-progress and negative results.

### H4 — Scientific Method Lens

A synchronized familiar/expanded/state view helps domain users understand Corpus Forge without perceiving it as a rejection of established scientific methods.

### H5 — Spatial environment

Spatial placement improves memory for artifact role, relation, and workflow stage for selected complex tasks.

### H6 — Spatial pointer

Progressive spatial closure reduces wrong-object selection and improves recovery in dense, occluded, or nested scenes.

### H7 — RTS operations

Multi-scale strategic views improve identification of blocked work and consequential next actions without encouraging low-quality throughput behavior.

## 3. Spatial pointer experiment

### Conditions

- A: nearest visible surface;
- B: ray cast plus explicit target list;
- C: volumetric candidate field, depth control, preview, and local binding.

### Tasks

- select a partially occluded claim;
- traverse a nested claim-to-source path;
- select a semantic group across depth;
- identify a restricted action;
- recover from an intentional mis-target;
- manipulate an object in its local frame.

### Measures

- accuracy;
- completion time;
- number of reversals;
- recovery time;
- confidence;
- NASA-TLX or comparable workload measure;
- learnability after repeated trials;
- visual/motion discomfort;
- preference with qualitative explanation.

### Promotion condition

The pointer remains an exploratory research result until a defined task set shows meaningful improvement without unacceptable workload or accessibility cost.

## 4. Publication and negative results

Every design hypothesis should be published internally with:

- question;
- prototype;
- method;
- result;
- limitations;
- status;
- next decision.

A failed UX hypothesis should be preserved as a negative result and folded into subsequent constraints.
