# Operational Plan: Boundary First Labs Living Research Environment

## 1. Executive objective

Build a coherent Boundary First Labs digital environment that meets users where they are, gives them an immediate institutional and domain-recognizable entrance, exposes the structure and maturity of the lab's work responsibly, and supports rigorous knowledge production through Corpus Forge.

The program should evolve through interoperable modules rather than a monolithic redesign. Every module must operate over a shared semantic and governance layer so that public web pages, Atlas views, dense workbench panels, scientific workflows, and immersive spatial environments remain projections of one governed research state.

## 2. North-star experience

A user enters through a familiar role or problem, recognizes objects and practices from their own domain, and is guided into one connected environment.

They can:

1. orient themselves in Boundary First Labs;
2. understand what the lab is and how it works;
3. explore research and products without first mastering Boundary Theory;
4. inspect the claim status, provenance, history, and limits of any artifact;
5. begin or join a bounded inquiry;
6. move between familiar scientific-method language and the fuller Corpus Forge cycle;
7. work through dense 2D tools or an inhabitable spatial environment;
8. understand where work is blocked, contradicted, under review, promoted, or reopened;
9. traverse continuously from one artifact to the complete Atlas;
10. preserve every consequential transition as durable, inspectable state.

## 4. Program architecture

The product program is divided into five layers.

### Layer A — Semantic research state

Canonical objects and relations:

- source;
- source anchor;
- question;
- boundary;
- invariant;
- assumption;
- definition;
- model or hypothesis;
- protocol;
- instrument;
- trace;
- result;
- claim;
- contradiction;
- negative result;
- repair;
- review;
- promotion;
- publication;
- research program;
- person, organization, and authority;
- disclosure and access state.

This layer must remain independent of any specific UI.

### Layer B — Governance and lawful transition

Defines:

- permitted state transitions;
- claim ceilings;
- disclosure classes;
- source and provenance requirements;
- critic review obligations;
- human promotion gates;
- reopening conditions;
- supersession and retirement;
- negative-result preservation;
- role and authority boundaries.

### Layer C — Binding and workflow profiles

Maps semantic objects and governed transitions into:

- domain vocabulary;
- workflow stages;
- UI component families;
- 2D layouts;
- spatial objects and rooms;
- pointer behaviors;
- alerts and status tokens;
- generated documentation and export formats.

### Layer D — Experience surfaces

- public front door;
- institutional site;
- guided Atlas;
- Corpus Forge workbench;
- Binding Protocol Studio;
- Scientific Method Lens;
- review and promotion interface;
- publication and negative-results archive;
- spatial workbench;
- RTS operational view.

### Layer E — connectors and execution

- GitHub canonical state;
- Google Drive review surfaces;
- structured local or hosted database;
- search/indexing;
- agent operator and critic runs;
- web publishing;
- optional 3D renderer;
- export and archival packaging.

## 5. Modular feature plan

### Module M00 — Shared semantic kernel

**Purpose:** Establish the source-independent object model and lawful transition grammar.

**MVP:** JSON Schema or equivalent definitions for the core object classes, relations, statuses, provenance, versioning, and authority.

**Why first:** Every later surface depends on this layer. UI work should not silently become the data model.

**Acceptance:** The same sample inquiry can render in a simple list, Atlas graph, workbench, and spatial mock without changing the underlying objects.

### Module M01 — Public institutional front door

**Purpose:** Answer “Who are you?”, “Why are you here?”, and “What is Boundary First Labs?” before introducing the full theory.

**MVP:** Six recognizable role entrances, adaptive institutional explanation, and links to the Lab, Work, Corpus Forge, and Explore.

**Improvement path:** Personalized paths, domain-specific visual vocabularies, saved orientation, and progressive disclosure.

### Module M02 — Responsible publication and claim surfaces

**Purpose:** Publish unfinished research, proof attempts, conjectures, negative results, and supported work without collapsing their epistemic status.

**MVP:** Artifact class, outcome, claim ceiling, provenance, current maturity, contradictions, and reopening conditions displayed on every research object.

**Improvement path:** Release histories, independent review records, reproduction links, and program-level research ledgers.

### Module M03 — Atlas redesign

**Purpose:** Provide a calm top-level cluster map, guided camera paths, and deep graph structure on demand.

**MVP:** Bubble-cluster overview, persistent inspector, breadcrumbs, minimap, search, path rail, and deeper typed-edge view.

**Improvement path:** User-specific guided paths, contribution views, temporal evolution, and spatial observatory projection.

### Module M04 — Corpus Forge living workbench

**Purpose:** Make jobs, sources, extracts, claims, contradictions, ledgers, critic review, and human promotion operationally visible.

**MVP:** Job intake, seven-stage Forge pipeline, source anchors, artifact cards, inspector, ledgers, review packet, critic report, and human gate.

**Improvement path:** Branch comparison, batch operations, agent execution visibility, collaborative review, and Atlas promotion.

### Module M05 — Binding Protocol Studio

**Purpose:** Specify interface behavior independently of source data through reusable schema-to-experience workflows.

**MVP:** Schema intake, desired outcome, workflow template, binding canvas, live preview, validation, and binding-file export.

**Improvement path:** Pattern marketplace/library, domain profile inheritance, 3D environmental bindings, test fixtures, and automated migration checks.

### Module M06 — Scientific Method Lens

**Purpose:** Preserve a familiar scientific-method entrance while revealing the broader lawful inquiry cycle.

**MVP:** Synchronized Familiar, Expanded Forge, and Research-State views with phase-specific artifact templates.

**Improvement path:** Method profiles for empirical, computational, mathematical, literature, engineering, and institutional inquiry.

### Module M07 — Spatial research environment

**Purpose:** Make inquiry feel inhabitable and make epistemic structure spatially legible.

**MVP:** One room—the Central Workbench—with artifacts, inspector, spatial navigation, and a transition to the Atlas.

**Improvement path:** Threshold, Instrument Gallery, Experiment Chamber, Correspondence Hall, Repair Forge, Review Chamber, Archive, and Observatory.

### Module M08 — Boundary-aware spatial pointer

**Purpose:** Replace flat nearest-surface selection with visible progressive closure over depth, semantics, and admissibility.

**MVP:** Volumetric candidate field, depth control, preview-before-commit, local-frame binding, and admissible-action menu.

**Improvement path:** Nested artifact traversal, group boundary selection, semantic scale changes, multi-device input, and personalized confidence calibration.

### Module M09 — RTS research-operations layer

**Purpose:** Support strategic, operational, and object-level understanding of a living research program.

**MVP:** Minimap, alerts, queues, active inquiries, blocked states, saved groups, and multi-scale zoom.

**Improvement path:** Agent assignments, resource/attention planning, research campaigns, replay, and scenario comparison.

### Module M10 — Agent operation and criticism

**Purpose:** Make AI-assisted synthesis visible, bounded, and reviewable.

**MVP:** Operator, Critic, and Human Promotion Gate; every run writes durable job artifacts.

**Improvement path:** Specialized bounded workers, explicit tool permissions, activity visualization, and reproducible agent runs.

### Module M11 — Accessibility and alternate projections

**Purpose:** Ensure the environment is not dependent on visual immersion or precise spatial control.

**MVP:** Full keyboard navigation, text/list projection, reduced motion, high contrast, screen-reader semantic structure, and direct search.

**Improvement path:** voice control, haptic cues, customizable density, and alternate cognitive modes.

## 6. Shared design laws

1. **Institution before doctrine.** Explain the lab and its commitments before demanding theoretical understanding.
2. **Recognizable objects before abstract taxonomy.** Meet users through familiar artifacts and practices.
3. **One governed state, many projections.** No UI becomes an independent source of truth.
4. **Progressive disclosure.** Top-level calm; depth and edges appear when requested.
5. **Status is structural.** Claim, evidence, disclosure, and authority state remain visible.
6. **Contradictions are first-class.** Do not hide them as errors or delete them during synthesis.
7. **Negative results are productive state transitions.** Preserve the route, failure, and resulting constraint.
8. **AI proposes; closure gates authorize.** Consequential promotion remains independent and human-governed.
9. **Spatial metaphor must be operational.** Every room, object, light, barrier, and path maps to system state.
10. **The environment cannot trap the user.** Search, direct navigation, and efficient workbench views remain available.
11. **No gamified rigor.** Avoid points, fake urgency, and volume incentives that distort research quality.
12. **Everything can be reopened.** Closure is bounded by purpose and carries explicit reopening conditions.

## 7. Integrated user journeys

### Journey A — Public visitor

```text
Role entrance
→ adaptive explanation of the Lab
→ recognizable project or on-ramp
→ guided path
→ Atlas reveal
→ inspect a publication and its claim status
→ follow or support the work
```

### Journey B — Researcher beginning an inquiry

```text
Select method profile
→ declare purpose and boundary
→ import sources
→ generate candidates
→ choose a test
→ preserve traces
→ compare and diagnose
→ repair or close
→ critic review
→ human promotion
→ Atlas / publication projection
```

### Journey C — Existing corpus maintainer

```text
Open job queue
→ inspect source and extraction deltas
→ identify contradiction
→ create repair branch
→ run critic
→ approve bounded promotion
→ update dependency graph and release history
```

### Journey D — Immersive spatial user

```text
Enter Threshold
→ arrive at Central Workbench
→ probe artifact field
→ bind a claim
→ inspect provenance
→ walk/transition to Correspondence Hall
→ compare observation and model
→ move repair to Forge
→ submit to Review Chamber
→ observe promoted artifact in Archive and Atlas
```

### Journey E — Strategic research director

```text
Open RTS overview
→ identify blocked research programs
→ inspect contradiction and review queues
→ select a bounded artifact group
→ assign an operator or human owner
→ monitor state transitions
→ intervene at authority gates
```

## 8. Deliverable strategy

The program should ship as a chain of vertical slices.

A vertical slice must include:

- canonical object definitions;
- binding profile;
- UI surface;
- sample data;
- state transitions;
- governance behavior;
- accessibility projection;
- test and review packet;
- exportable artifact.

The first recommended slice is:

> One bounded research inquiry rendered in the 2D Corpus Forge workbench, the Atlas, the Scientific Method Lens, and a single-room spatial Central Workbench, all driven by one binding file.

## 9. Success measures

### Product comprehension

- Visitors can explain what Boundary First Labs is without first explaining Boundary Theory.
- Users can distinguish a source, inference, claim, negative result, and supported result.
- Users can locate the next lawful action for an inquiry.

### Operational integrity

- Every displayed claim reaches a source or declared inference.
- UI projections do not create divergent state.
- Failed and superseded states remain recoverable.
- Promotion cannot occur without the required gate.

### Interaction quality

- Users can move from strategic to object scale without losing context.
- Spatial selection reduces wrong-object selection in dense or occluded scenes.
- Efficient non-spatial workflows remain faster for routine operations.

### Institutional trust

- Publication pages expose status, limits, and history.
- Negative results remain visible and useful.
- Agent activity and authority boundaries are inspectable.
