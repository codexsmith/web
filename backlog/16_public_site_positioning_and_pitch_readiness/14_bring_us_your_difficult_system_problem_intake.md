## Purpose

The website now has increasingly strong answers to:

- What is Boundary First Labs?
- What does it research?
- What has it built?
- Why should it be capitalized?
- How does its work become transferable capability?

It still needs one direct invitation for the visitor who is not primarily a reader, funder, investor, or reviewer.

That visitor has a system that is already difficult.

It may be overloaded, brittle, opaque, exception-ridden, politically constrained, technically tangled, maintained by invisible labor, or impossible to change without breaking something else.

They do not necessarily know which BFL product, method, theory, or service applies.

They know this:

> **We have a complicated problem. The current representation is not helping us solve it.**

The website should give that visitor a first-class entrance.

# What this layer is — and is not

This should not become a generic consulting page saying BFL can solve anything.

It is a **problem-intake projection** for situations where the immediate task is to determine what the real bounded problem is before choosing a service, product, research program, or partner.

The intake promise is:

> **We will help reconstruct the system well enough to decide what kind of problem it is, what evidence matters, what BFL can responsibly contribute, and what should happen next.**

The intake does **not** promise:

- that BFL has domain authority in every field;
- that every problem belongs inside the Lab;
- that every problem should become a software project;
- that every engagement should become a long-term consulting relationship;
- that the Boundary First vocabulary is automatically the correct model;
- that a diagnosis guarantees a repair;
- legal, clinical, regulatory, financial, safety, or other professional authority BFL does not possess;
- that the appropriate next step must be performed by BFL.

A successful intake may conclude:

- this is a bounded engineering problem BFL can work on;
- this is an audit/pilot candidate;
- this is a research question;
- this needs a domain expert or partner first;
- this should be decomposed into a smaller problem;
- the current evidence is insufficient;
- there is no responsible intervention yet;
- BFL is not the right steward.

That is still useful closure.

# Why this belongs in the Lab Machine

The invitation is the inverse of the capital projection.

The capital projection asks:

> **What additional capacity should enter the Lab?**

The problem-intake projection asks:

> **What difficult reality should enter the Lab's inquiry machinery?**

The shared mechanism is:

`Input -> Boundary -> Representation -> Evidence -> Work Type -> Intervention -> Witness -> Repair / Transfer`

Capital and problem intake should therefore appear as two different input manifolds into the same machine.

### CAPITAL input

Provides capacity:

- time;
- engineering;
- validation;
- distribution;
- stewardship;
- infrastructure.

### PROBLEM input

Provides a consequence-bearing system:

- current state;
- actors;
- artifacts;
- constraints;
- pain/defect;
- authority;
- evidence;
- consequences;
- desired change.

Neither input bypasses admissibility, evidence, domain authority, claim ceilings, or repair obligations.

# The problems we are inviting

The interface should use recognizable failure patterns rather than industry categories alone.

## Overloaded representation

Symptoms:

- one spreadsheet, dashboard, service, workflow, or person carries too many meanings;
- important distinctions are encoded as conventions only insiders remember;
- the representation is locally efficient but globally confusing;
- different teams use the same object to mean different things.

Prompt:

> **Too much reality has been compressed into one representation. What did it forget?**

## Exception-built system

Symptoms:

- the happy path looks clean but actual operation is dominated by workarounds;
- policies and implementation disagree;
- edge cases have become the normal case;
- hidden labor absorbs defects.

Prompt:

> **Show us the exceptions. They often reveal the real system.**

## Responsibility gap

Symptoms:

- authority can act but nobody can say where responsibility lands;
- multiple teams own pieces, nobody owns the consequence;
- affected people cannot find a meaningful contest or repair path;
- escalation routes end in another handoff.

Prompt:

> **Who can act, who is affected, and who can actually repair the result?**

## Brittle software / inherited system

Symptoms:

- nobody wants to change it;
- every fix breaks an unrelated path;
- business rules exist in code, tickets, memory, and production incidents simultaneously;
- interfaces conceal state rather than expose it.

Prompt:

> **Before rewriting it, let's recover the object the software is trying to represent.**

## Maintenance / lifecycle debt

Symptoms:

- launch succeeded but ownership disappeared;
- deferred work accumulates outside the metrics used to judge success;
- temporary human processes quietly became permanent infrastructure;
- replacement, retirement, or handoff has no owner.

Prompt:

> **What must continually be supplied for this capability to remain real?**

## Evidence / knowledge overload

Symptoms:

- reports, chats, documents, dashboards, and AI output multiply faster than conclusions can be trusted;
- provenance disappears during synthesis;
- contradictions are hard to recover;
- nobody knows which version or claim is authoritative.

Prompt:

> **The problem may not be information scarcity. It may be governed continuity.**

## Cross-boundary failure

Symptoms:

- each department or component works locally, but the whole fails at handoffs;
- state, meaning, authority, or responsibility changes at interfaces;
- the defect is always “someone else's part.”

Prompt:

> **Let's inspect the crossing, not just the components on either side.**

## Wicked / unstable problem

Symptoms:

- defining the problem changes it;
- interventions create new actors and constraints;
- there is no clean single objective;
- every solution exports consequence somewhere else.

Prompt:

> **Start with the present boundary, preserve uncertainty, act narrowly, observe consequence, and update the representation.**

# Intake grammar

The visitor should not be forced to understand BFL terminology before beginning.

Use ordinary questions first.

## First pass — tell us what is happening

1. **What is happening?**
2. **What makes this difficult now?**
3. **Who is involved?**
4. **Who is affected?**
5. **What have you already tried?**
6. **What seems to break when you change it?**
7. **What absolutely must remain true?**
8. **What evidence or artifacts exist?**
9. **Who can authorize a change?**
10. **Who owns repair if the change causes harm or failure?**
11. **What would a meaningfully better state look like?**
12. **What are we not allowed to assume?**

This is deliberately recognizable language.

The system can translate responses into deeper Boundary First structures after intake.

# Internal routing projection

The intake should route a problem into one of several bounded next-step classes.

## RECONSTRUCT

Use when the main defect is that nobody has a trustworthy model of the system.

Likely outputs:

- system map;
- actor/artifact map;
- state/transition map;
- boundary/interface inventory;
- invariant list;
- unknowns and evidence gaps.

Possible BFL connections:

- Boundary-First Engineering;
- Software Before Code;
- systems reconstruction;
- Corpus Forge for source-heavy problems.

## AUDIT

Use when authority, consequence, contestability, responsibility, or repair are central.

Likely outputs:

- agency map;
- representation map;
- consequence chain;
- contest path;
- repair ownership map;
- defect register.

Possible BFL connection:

- Agency + Representation Audit.

## BUILD / REPAIR

Use when the bounded representation is sufficiently understood and an instrument or implementation can be constructed or repaired.

Likely outputs:

- prototype;
- reference implementation;
- interface/workbench;
- workflow redesign;
- instrumentation;
- integration;
- repair plan.

Possible BFL connections:

- Boundary-First Engineering;
- BFUX;
- software/product work.

## TEST / VALIDATE

Use when the core need is to make a claim, model, intervention, or representation answer to reality.

Likely outputs:

- baseline;
- benchmark;
- experiment;
- pilot charter;
- review packet;
- reproduction attempt;
- failure conditions.

Possible BFL connections:

- Research;
- witness-domain testbeds;
- external domain collaboration.

## GOVERN / STEWARD

Use when the problem is less about initial construction than ownership, maintenance, authority, versioning, retirement, or transfer.

Likely outputs:

- authority map;
- stewardship contract;
- maintenance path;
- release/promotion rules;
- correction/supersession process;
- transfer/retirement plan.

Possible BFL connections:

- Governance;
- Service Bus;
- institutional-capacity model.

## REFER / PARTNER

Use when BFL can help bound the system but the next responsible step requires expertise, authority, infrastructure, or standing elsewhere.

Likely outputs:

- bounded problem statement;
- missing-capability declaration;
- partner/expert requirements;
- evidence package for handoff;
- explicit non-claim about BFL authority.

This is a successful outcome, not a failed sale.

## DECLINE / STOP

Use when:

- the requested intervention would exceed authority;
- evidence is too weak;
- consequences cannot be responsibly bounded;
- the relationship would require false claims;
- the work is outside BFL's competence and no lawful partnership path exists;
- the prospective client/funder requires the Lab to hide evidence, responsibility, or affected parties.

The site does not need to dramatize this path, but the system should encode it.

# The first engagement should be diagnostic

The website should resist jumping directly from a visitor's story to a large implementation proposal.

Preferred first engagement pattern:

`Problem Intake -> Bounded Reconstruction -> Findings / Defect Map -> Recommended Work Type -> Go / Partner / Stop`

Possible public offer names:

- Difficult System Diagnostic
- Boundary Reconstruction
- System Legibility Sprint
- Boundary & Consequence Map
- Problem Framing / System Reconstruction

Do not over-name too early. The core promise matters more than product packaging:

> **Before we sell you a solution, let's make sure we are solving the right system.**

# Native apparatus treatment

The interface should look like an **intake bay** or **diagnostic port** on the Lab Machine.

Suggested sequence:

### 01 — LOAD

Visitor supplies the messy reality.

### 02 — BOUND

Identify what is inside the immediate inquiry, what is outside, and what cannot be ignored.

### 03 — TRACE

Actors, artifacts, authority, state, evidence, consequence, dependencies.

### 04 — FIND THE DEFECT

What fails to close? What is overloaded, missing, displaced, contradictory, ownerless, or maintained by invisible labor?

### 05 — ROUTE

Reconstruct / Audit / Build / Validate / Govern / Partner / Stop.

### 06 — DEFINE THE WITNESS

What would tell us that the next intervention actually helped?

### 07 — REPAIR / TRANSFER

Perform bounded work or hand it to the right steward with the representation intact.

Visual principle:

> The visitor should feel that they are **loading a real system into an instrument**, not filling out a sales-lead form.

# Relationship to the capital narrative

This action layer improves the capital case too.

A credible lab should not merely have a theory about difficult systems. It should have a front door through which difficult systems can become:

- bounded inquiries;
- paid diagnostics;
- pilots;
- research questions;
- case studies;
- validation environments;
- product requirements;
- partnerships;
- negative results;
- reusable methods.

That creates an institutional feedback loop:

`Real Problems -> Diagnostic Work -> Evidence -> Method Repair -> Better Instruments -> New Capability -> New Problems`

Capital increases the Lab's capacity to receive and close more of these loops without lowering its evidence or stewardship standards.

This is a strong bridge between funder narrative and earned-revenue narrative.

---

# Evidence / claim boundary

The website may safely claim that BFL invites bounded problem intake and that its methods are designed to reconstruct difficult systems.

It should not claim, without evidence:

- guaranteed resolution;
- universal cross-domain expertise;
- superior outcomes across industries;
- regulatory/compliance authority;
- legal/clinical/financial professional advice;
- a large consulting team;
- immediate capacity for every submitted problem;
- that every intake becomes an engagement;
- that a BFL diagnosis proves Boundary Theory.

Each eventual engagement should have:

- scope;
- authority boundary;
- evidence requirements;
- deliverables;
- claim ceiling;
- closure event;
- repair/exit path.
