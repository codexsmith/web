# Glossary and Failure Patterns

## Canonical working terms

### Boundary

A consequential separation that changes which entities, states, transformations, responsibilities, or claims are admissible.

### Closure

A state in which a represented need or claim has passed through its declared operating process, encountered consequence, absorbed material discrepancy, and produced an output the relevant domain can recognize, verify, and use.

### Consequence channel

A mechanism through which reality can answer a claim or action: test, measurement, telemetry, user behavior, reconciliation, experiment, formal check, audit, or other discriminating observation.

### Consequence-bearing artifact

An artifact that is connected to declared expectations, observation channels, repair obligations, and closure conditions.

### Bounded consequential circuit

A finite, explicit loop connecting representation, action, observation, discrepancy, repair, verification, and closure.

### Correctability

The capacity of a person, process, model, or system to receive valid evidence and revise without requiring denial or collapse.

### Repair

An accountable modification to the representation, implementation, process, boundary, authority assignment, or instrumentation responsible for a discrepancy.

### Verification

The rerunning or application of a declared consequence channel after repair.

### Independent consequence channel

An observation path not merely derived from the same generative process that produced the claim.

### Claim contract

A typed record of what is asserted, its source, assumptions, scope, expected consequence, failure condition, and permitted wording.

### Closure contract

The declared conditions under which a circuit may be considered closed.

### Repair authority

The bounded permission to modify a particular object or system in response to discrepancy.

### Accepted open risk

A known unresolved discrepancy or hazard with an explicit owner, boundary, rationale, review condition, and consequence exposure.

## Mediation terms

### Social mediation

Coordination and evaluation through language, reputation, hierarchy, institutions, media, incentives, and shared representations.

### Consequence mediation

Correction through actual effects under operating conditions.

### Categorical smuggling

Undeclared transport of a claim across a category boundary while retaining unearned authority or implication.

### Representational laundering

The acquisition of legitimacy by passing a weakly supported claim through a stronger-seeming frame.

## Failure patterns

### Representational closure

The artifact sounds or appears complete, so the system treats it as closed without a consequence test.

### Recursive confirmation

AI-generated or institutionally generated output is validated mainly by derivative output from the same mediation system.

### False closure

A circuit is marked closed although its stated consequence, verification, or downstream recognition has not occurred.

### Closure laundering

A ticket state, report, dashboard, approval, merge, publication, or milestone is used to imply an outcome never independently verified.

### Displaced consequence

The generating team receives credit while users, operators, future maintainers, the public, or another system receives the failure cost.

### Ceremonial repair

The system discusses, documents, or performs concern about a defect without modifying the structure that reproduces it.

### Unowned discrepancy

Evidence shows mismatch, but no role has both responsibility and authority to repair it.

### Category collapse

Distinct states, actors, authorities, evidence classes, or obligations are compressed into one label or field.

### Authority collapse

Technical capacity, model confidence, organizational status, or access is treated as legitimate permission to act.

### Instrumentation capture

A proxy metric becomes the target while the protected consequence degrades.

### Repair displacement

A repair removes a visible symptom by moving cost or defect across a boundary.

### Model insulation

Contradictory evidence is reinterpreted to preserve the representation rather than update it.

### Social override

A causally warranted conclusion is rejected or weakened primarily because it violates hierarchy, consensus, tone, or expected posture.
