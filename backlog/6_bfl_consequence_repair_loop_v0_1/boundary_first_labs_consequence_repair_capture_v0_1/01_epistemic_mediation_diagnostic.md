# Epistemic Mediation Diagnostic

## Core proposition

A sophisticated deception often does not falsify a sentence directly. It falsifies the **kind of sentence**, object, authority, evidence, scale, or implication the audience is asked to perceive.

> **A lie falsifies content. Propaganda often falsifies category.**

The result is truth in false clothing or falsehood in truth's clothing.

## The structural defect

An illicit categorical transport has the form:

```text
object or claim in category A
        ↓ concealed reassignment
presented as category B
        ↓ borrowed authority or implication
licensed response C
```

Examples include:

- preference presented as necessity;
- prediction presented as observation;
- correlation presented as cause;
- policy consequence presented as natural inevitability;
- institutional power presented as neutral procedure;
- model output presented as measurement;
- generated coherence presented as proof.

## Working vocabulary

### Category error

A claim, object, operation, or relation is treated as belonging to a category whose rules do not apply to it.

### Categorical smuggling

A claim crosses a boundary without declaring the transformation, while retaining implications valid only in its prior or borrowed category.

### Representational laundering

A political interest, commercial incentive, emotional reaction, unsupported conjecture, or institutional preference passes through a legitimating representation—such as science, safety, neutrality, tradition, consensus, or inevitability—and exits with authority it did not earn.

### Social mediation

The layer of language, reputation, hierarchy, media, institutions, incentives, and shared representations through which people coordinate and evaluate one another.

### Consequence mediation

Correction through the actual effects of an action, model, or system under its operating conditions.

## The mediation inversion

The ordinary corrective loop is:

```text
perception → action → material consequence → correction
```

A heavily mediated loop can become:

```text
representation → social validation → coordinated action
→ displaced or delayed consequence → narrative repair
```

This does not abolish consequence. It changes who receives it, how quickly it returns, and whether its causal source remains legible.

A system becomes epistemically dangerous when it can preserve social coherence while remaining materially wrong.

## Diagnostic questions

When evaluating a claim, decision, process, or institution, ask:

1. What kind of thing is this presented as?
2. What kind of thing is it actually?
3. Which authority, innocence, urgency, or implication is imported by the category assignment?
4. Which distinctions were collapsed or omitted?
5. What independent consequence channel can answer the claim?
6. Who receives the consequence if the claim is wrong?
7. Can the responsible representation be repaired, or only defended?

## The AI-mediated delusion loop

AI can intensify mediation because it can provide fluent, responsive, coherent elaboration without adding independent evidence.

```text
intuition
  → AI elaboration
  → felt coherence
  → increased conviction
  → further prompting
  → stronger elaboration
```

The controlling question is:

> **Did the claim gain support, or did it only gain language?**

### Warning signs

- the framework explains every objection;
- the same conversation is treated as multiple confirmations;
- missing evidence is reclassified as suppression or inability to understand;
- contradictions are absorbed rhetorically rather than resolved;
- no observation could force revision;
- AI-generated support is cited as though independent;
- the idea becomes more detailed without becoming more testable;
- closure is declared from coherence alone.

### Required safeguards

A responsible human–AI research loop should require:

- **Provability:** what can be derived or decisively checked?
- **Repeatability:** can another person reproduce the result without inheriting the conversation?
- **Transport:** does the claim survive another notation, implementation, dataset, model, or context?
- **Evidence:** which observations discriminate it from a compelling story?
- **Falsifiability:** what would force revision or abandonment?
- **Independence:** is support external to the generative loop?
- **Provenance:** which source supports each consequential statement?
- **Claim ceiling:** how strongly may the result be stated?

## Epistemic moat, walls, and access doors

The epistemic boundaries are not private barriers invented to exclude others. They are the visible edges of consequence.

- The **moat** is the cost of crossing from assertion to warranted claim.
- The **guardrails** preserve valid inference and keep category transitions explicit.
- The **walls** prevent unsupported transport between domains.
- The **doors** are lawful access procedures: study, evidence, demonstration, correction, replication, and earned trust.

The decisive difference is not whether a person begins outside. Everyone does. It is whether, after encountering the boundary, they learn the door or attack the wall.

## Claim discipline

The social-mediation thesis is a powerful analysis frame, not yet a completed empirical theory. Public or academic use should distinguish:

- direct observation;
- professional case history;
- formal systems analogy;
- institutional interpretation;
- evolutionary conjecture;
- validated social-science result.
