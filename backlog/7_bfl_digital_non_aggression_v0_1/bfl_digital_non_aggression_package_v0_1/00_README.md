# Digital Non-Aggression / Non-Destruction / Non-Interference
## Boundary First Labs Working Package v0.1

**Date:** 2026-08-01  
**Status:** Internal research and doctrine seed  
**Project lane:** AI governance / digital systems / agency and public consequence  
**Source basis:** The Boundary First Labs conversation concerning Morris-II and adaptive AI-agent worms, cross-checked against the cited primary research papers and aligned with the existing corpus governance lane.

## Purpose

This package converts a short project conversation into a bounded research and development program for a possible **Digital Non-Aggression, Non-Destruction, and Non-Interference Accord**.

The package does **not** claim that a completed treaty, legal doctrine, or accepted international norm already exists. It separates:

- verified technical demonstrations;
- Boundary First Labs interpretations;
- normative proposals;
- candidate terminology;
- unresolved legal and policy questions;
- operational tasks and promotion gates.

## Central working proposition

Connected digital systems increasingly permit attacks to propagate through delegated agency, trusted context, tool access, shared infrastructure, and automated communication. Local security controls remain necessary, but they may be insufficient when harmful operations can reproduce, adapt, appropriate compute, and cross organizational or national boundaries without continuous human direction.

The project therefore asks:

> What minimum prohibitions, duties, technical controls, accountability rules, and repair guarantees are required to prevent digital systems from becoming unconsenting substrates for automated aggression, destruction, or interference?

## Existing corpus relationship

The refined corpus already contains an AI governance sequence leading from **AI as Forge** through bounded agency, closure certification, and a non-aggression or treaty lane. Existing audits describe the non-aggression frame as promising but caution that:

- “aggression” is legally and politically loaded;
- treaty-scale doctrine should not be the first public governance artifact;
- definitions, scope limits, legal comparisons, and policy grounding are required;
- the governance lane should proceed from craft and agency into law and institutional design rather than beginning with maximal doctrine.

This package therefore treats the Accord as a **research program and policy architecture**, not a finished declaration.

## Recommended use

Use this package to:

- establish a canonical project folder;
- commission legal and cybersecurity research;
- build a threat and incident corpus;
- conduct structured expert review;
- derive a shorter concept paper;
- prototype technical controls and certification criteria;
- decide whether “accord,” “protocol,” “convention,” “compact,” or “treaty” is the correct institutional form.

## Promotion rule

Do not promote the project to a public treaty proposal until the following are complete:

1. terminology and scope review;
2. comparative legal and norms analysis;
3. security expert review;
4. at least three fully worked case studies;
5. an exception and defensive-action framework;
6. a responsibility and attribution model;
7. a technical verification and compliance model;
8. an explicit claim ledger separating current law, emerging norm, and Boundary First Labs proposal.
