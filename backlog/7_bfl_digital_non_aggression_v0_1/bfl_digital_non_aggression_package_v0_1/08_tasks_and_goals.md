# Tasks and Goals Registry

## Program goal

Develop a rigorous, evidence-grounded Boundary First Labs framework for preventing and governing autonomous or automated digital aggression, destruction, and interference, with corresponding technical controls, responsibility structures, and repair requirements.

## Non-goals for v0.1

- declaring a finished treaty;
- asserting a new category of international law;
- treating all cyber incidents as aggression;
- assigning sovereign status to AI systems;
- publishing exploit-enabling technical details;
- proposing universal surveillance or centralized control of all models;
- replacing ordinary cybersecurity engineering.

## Goal G1 — Validate the technical threat class

**Outcome:** Accurate account of demonstrated self-propagating and adaptive agentic threats.

### Tasks

- [ ] Read and annotate the full Morris-II paper and final conference version.
- [ ] Read and annotate the adaptive-worm preprint and associated research site.
- [ ] Add ClawWorm and other relevant 2025–2026 agent-propagation research where appropriate.
- [ ] Separate prompt/RAG worms, runtime agent worms, adaptive network worms, and conventional malware.
- [ ] Record assumptions, environments, success metrics, and limitations.
- [ ] Build a defense and mitigation matrix.
- [ ] Obtain independent cybersecurity review.

### Deliverables

- technical literature review;
- threat taxonomy;
- evidence matrix;
- defense matrix;
- reviewed case-study packet.

### Exit gate

A security reviewer can reproduce the project’s factual claims from primary sources and finds no material exaggeration.

## Goal G2 — Define the protected invariants and prohibited transitions

**Outcome:** A precise boundary-first grammar for aggression, destruction, interference, propagation, delegation, and repair.

### Tasks

- [ ] Stabilize “authorized-purpose integrity.”
- [ ] Define non-propagation, consequence ceiling, responsibility continuity, and repairability.
- [ ] Specify admissible and inadmissible transitions.
- [ ] Create defect classes and closure conditions.
- [ ] Test the grammar against at least six cases.
- [ ] Identify where existing security terminology already suffices.
- [ ] Remove redundant or purely rhetorical concepts.

### Deliverables

- definitions document;
- typed actor/operation model;
- case encodings;
- glossary;
- rejected-terms register.

### Exit gate

The framework adds diagnostic value beyond renaming familiar attack categories.

## Goal G3 — Map current law and norms

**Outcome:** Clear separation of existing prohibition, emerging norm, proposed extension, and open gap.

### Tasks

- [ ] Review cybercrime and unauthorized-access regimes.
- [ ] Review international-law scholarship on sovereignty and prohibited intervention.
- [ ] Review use-of-force and armed-attack thresholds without collapsing them into the project’s broader moral use of aggression.
- [ ] Review state responsibility and attribution.
- [ ] Review AI liability, negligence, product safety, and intermediary responsibility.
- [ ] Review confidence-building measures, cyber norms, and arms-control analogies.
- [ ] Review responsible disclosure and security-research protections.
- [ ] Obtain qualified legal review.

### Deliverables

- current-law map;
- doctrine/proposal comparison table;
- terminology risk memo;
- legal claim ledger;
- expert review notes.

### Exit gate

Every public legal statement is tagged as current law, contested interpretation, emerging norm, or proposal.

## Goal G4 — Design the responsibility and exception architecture

**Outcome:** Practical accountability without impossible or indiscriminate liability.

### Tasks

- [ ] Build a responsibility matrix across principal, developer, provider, deployer, operator, platform, agent, and intermediary.
- [ ] Define minimum deployer and operator duties by risk tier.
- [ ] Develop controlled exceptions for research, defense, emergency containment, and evidence preservation.
- [ ] Test necessity, proportionality, minimization, logging, review, and termination criteria.
- [ ] Model attribution uncertainty and shared responsibility.
- [ ] Define notification, remedy, and recertification duties.

### Deliverables

- responsibility matrix;
- exception protocol;
- incident-duty checklist;
- remedy and recertification model.

### Exit gate

The framework handles both malicious deployment and accidental escape, while preserving legitimate security research and defense.

## Goal G5 — Build a technical control and certification profile

**Outcome:** Testable controls corresponding to the normative framework.

### Tasks

- [ ] Define provenance and trust-boundary controls for retrieval and memory.
- [ ] Define propagation, destination, rate, and recursion limits.
- [ ] Define least-privilege and tool-authorization controls.
- [ ] Define emergency stop, credential revocation, and isolation controls.
- [ ] Define evidence, audit, and restoration requirements.

- test protocol;

- pilot report;

### Exit gate

- [ ] Build an abuse-case register.

- [ ] Review privacy and civil-liberties effects of logging and verification.
- [ ] Review burdens on small organizations and independent researchers.
- [ ] Review protest, journalism, interoperability, and public-interest research scenarios.
- [ ] Invite civil-society and digital-rights critique.

### Deliverables

- abuse-case ledger;
- proportionality analysis;
- revised safeguards.

### Exit gate

## Goal G9 — Decide the institutional pathway

**Outcome:** Evidence-based choice among protocol, certification, compact, model law, convention, or treaty.

### Tasks

- [ ] Compare institutional forms by authority, speed, verification, adoption, abuse risk, and enforceability.
- [ ] Identify the smallest viable first commitment.
- [ ] Design a layered adoption path.
- [ ] Define metrics of practical success.
- [ ] Reject “treaty” if the analysis does not justify it.

### Deliverables

- institutional-options report;
- adoption roadmap;
- minimum viable commitment;
- decision memo.

### Exit gate

The chosen form follows from the problem and evidence rather than rhetorical scale.
