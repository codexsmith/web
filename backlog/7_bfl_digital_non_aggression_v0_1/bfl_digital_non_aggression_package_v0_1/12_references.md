# References and Corpus Alignment

## Primary technical sources

### Morris-II

Stav Cohen, Ron Bitton, and Ben Nassi. **“Here Comes The AI Worm: Unleashing Zero-click Worms that Target GenAI-Powered Applications.”** arXiv:2403.02817, first submitted March 5, 2024.

- Abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02817
- Project/research materials: https://github.com/StavC/Here-Comes-the-AI-Worm

Use for:

- adversarial self-replicating prompts;
- indirect prompt-injection cascades;
- RAG-based persistence and propagation;
- confidential-data extraction in the experimental email-assistant ecosystem;
- proposed guardrail evaluation.

Do not use as proof that all agent systems are vulnerable or that treaty law is required.

### Adaptive AI-agent worms

Jonas Guan, Tom Blanchard, Hanna Foerster, Hengrui Jia, Gabriel Huang, and Nicolas Papernot. **“AI Agents Enable Adaptive Computer Worms.”** arXiv:2606.03811, submitted June 2, 2026.

- Abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03811
- Research overview: https://cleverhans.io/worm.html

Use for:

- target-specific attack strategy generation;
- adaptive propagation across heterogeneous hosts;
- use of compromised infrastructure for reasoning or reach;
- reduced relevance of centralized model-service controls for local open-weight operation;
- controlled testbed evidence.

Do not generalize beyond the experimental setup without reviewing the full methods and limitations.

## Citation and publication rule

Every public artifact should maintain three visibly separate source classes:

1. **Primary technical evidence** — what a study actually demonstrated.
2. **Boundary First Labs interpretation** — what the result suggests about boundaries, agency, propagation, or repair.
3. **Normative proposal** — what actors ought to prohibit, require, certify, or remedy.

No transition between these classes should be implicit.
