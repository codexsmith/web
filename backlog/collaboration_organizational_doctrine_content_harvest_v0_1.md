# Collaboration and organizational doctrine content harvest v0.1

**Date:** 2026-07-29  
**Status:** implementation-ready editorial brief; source promotion and public wording still require human review  
**Scope:** `BFL - Boundary Theory/boundary_theory_5_0_0/foundational_working_set` and `Lab V2`  
**Primary site surfaces:** `/collaborate`, `/about`, `/work`, `/business`, the guided first passage, and partner/critic audience routes

## Outcome

The source material supports a clear public doctrine:

> Boundary First Labs collaborates by declaring boundaries rather than erasing
> them. Shared purpose does not silently merge roles, authority, ownership,
> consent, or endorsement. Work preserves provenance and disagreement, places
> consequential decisions behind explicit gates, and defines repair,
> stewardship, handoff, and retirement before dependence accumulates.

This is stronger and more distinctive than generic language about partnership,
innovation, consulting, or community. It also supplies concrete content for
several site shells that are already built.

The fastest implementation path is:

1. expose the substantial collaboration content already present but unused in
   `src/content/nodes.json`;
2. add a compact "how we work" and "how work ends" sequence to
   `/collaborate`;
3. add role-bounded authority, anti-capture, repair capacity, and succession to
   the corresponding `/about` closure layers;
4. replace generic enterprise language on `/business` with the bounded
   institutional-refactoring method;
5. add one concise artifact-centered human-AI collaboration block to `/work`
   or `/collaborate`.

No source reviewed here establishes that every proposed governance mechanism
is already adopted, staffed, or proven in operation. Public presentation must
keep doctrine, policy proposal, current operating fact, and evidence of use
visibly distinct.

## Source status and evidence rule

| Source class | What it supports | What it does not support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Working institutional doctrine | Public explanation of intended principles and boundaries | Formal adoption, legal force, or demonstrated performance |
| Operating charter or role map | Current declared operating posture and named decision structure | Independent governance or filled roles not actually present |
| Gate, MOU, or checklist | A concrete mechanism the lab can use | Proof that it has been used in a completed collaboration |
| Self-application suite | Proposed anti-capture and continuity requirements | A claim that all mechanisms are presently operational |
| Chat reference | A strong formulation or design hypothesis | Canonical status or independent validation |
| Website `nodes.json` | Current public content source | External evidence merely because a field exists |

Editorial labels should use:

- **Current practice** only where an operating record exists;
- **Working doctrine** for promoted institutional principles still pending
  formal adoption;
- **Proposed mechanism** for recusal, appeal, succession, or review structures
  not yet demonstrably operational;
- **Evidence needed** where the site would otherwise imply completed use.

## High-signal doctrine and evidence snapshots

### COLLAB-001 — Boundaries enable collaboration

**Key point:** A collaboration becomes coherent when it distinguishes roles,
materials, authority, contribution, disclosure, completion, stewardship, and
exit. Boundary declaration is not bureaucratic overhead; it prevents ambiguity
from becoming transferred labor or risk.

**Evidence snapshot:** "Boundaries are not barriers to collaboration; they are
conditions that make collaboration coherent."

**Source:** `BFL - Boundary Theory/boundary_theory_5_0_0/foundational_working_set/governance_and_principles/institutional_principles.md`,
lines 142-159.

**Best slots:** `/collaborate` hero support copy; partner audience route;
collaboration-mode introduction.

### COLLAB-002 — A shared purpose does not merge roles or rights

**Key point:** Participation, advice, review, funding, access, authorship,
ownership, authorization, consent, and endorsement are separate relations.
None should be silently inferred from another.

**Evidence snapshot:** "Participation does not imply endorsement. Consultation
does not confer veto unless declared. Silence does not automatically establish
consent."

**Source:** `BFL - Boundary Theory/boundary_theory_5_0_0/foundational_working_set/governance_and_principles/institutional_principles.md`,
lines 183-201.

**Best slots:** existing `/collaborate` role-and-authority firewall; public
collaboration record; intake confirmation.

### COLLAB-003 — Collaboration needs a declaration package

**Key point:** Substantial work should declare purpose, parties, scope,
materials, roles, decision rights, provenance, claim ceiling, disclosure,
labor, conflict, repair, credit, exit, archive, and stewardship.

**Evidence snapshot:** The proposed declaration package is explicitly scalable:
lightweight for low-consequence work and more formal for research,
employment, deployment, public claims, or products.

**Source:** `BFL - Boundary Theory/boundary_theory_5_0_0/foundational_working_set/governance_and_principles/institutional_principles.md`,

**Operational corroboration:** `Lab V2/04_partnerships/collaboration_mou_outline.md`,

### COLLAB-004 — Provenance includes contribution, credit, and dissent

**Evidence snapshot:** "The institution should prefer legible disagreement to

**Key point:** Partner prestige, funding, expert proximity, or repeated
machine-generated synthesis does not promote a claim. Promotion depends on
domain-appropriate evidence and review.

**Evidence snapshot:** "A publication or collaborative artifact is promoted

### ORG-001 — Responsibility may be distributed, not dissolved

**Key point:** Consequential work should show the chain from purpose and
authorization through action, evidence, affected scope, contest, repair owner,

capacity, evidence access, and a route to reassessment.

**Evidence snapshot:** "Responsibility may be distributed, but it may not be

**Source:** `BFL - Boundary Theory/boundary_theory_5_0_0/foundational_working_set/governance_and_principles/institutional_principles.md`,

### ORG-002 — Repair is infrastructure

**Key point:** Repair may include correction, acknowledgement, appeal,

decision to stop. A route without an owner, authority, evidence, resources,
and a target state is not a real repair route.

**Evidence snapshot:** "Repair capacity should be tested before

**Source:** `BFL - Boundary Theory/boundary_theory_5_0_0/foundational_working_set/governance_and_principles/institutional_principles.md`,

## Slot-ready public copy

The following drafts are compressed from the sources above. They should carry
the label **Working collaboration doctrine** until formally adopted.

### `/collaborate` — hero support

Shared purpose is not permission to merge roles, sources, authority, rights, or
responsibility. We begin by declaring what is shared, who may decide, what
evidence counts, how contribution and disagreement remain visible, and how the
work can pause, transfer, or end.

### `/collaborate` — How we work

**Declare the boundary.** Name the purpose, people, materials, included work,
excluded work, claim ceiling, and conditions for review.

**Separate role from authority.** Participation, advice, review, funding,
access, authorship, ownership, approval, and endorsement remain distinct.

**Preserve provenance and dissent.** Record what came from whom, what changed,
what was independently checked, and what remains disputed.

**Create evidence, not borrowed authority.** A partner's name, prestige, or
funding does not promote a claim. Results pass the evidence gates appropriate
to their domain.

**Keep responsibility inspectable.** AI and automation may assist bounded
operations, but consequential judgment, release, correction, and repair retain
named human or institutional owners.

**Design closure before dependence.** Agree how support, rights, materials,
credit, correction, handoff, archive, and retirement will work before the
relationship becomes difficult to unwind.

### `/collaborate` — Evidence, not endorsement

A collaboration earns value by exposing work to use, criticism, comparison,
and failure. Where relevant, its record should include a useful case, a null or
limiting case, unresolved disagreement, source and transformation provenance,
predeclared outcome criteria, limitations, and the next decision: test, repair,
hold, promote, withdraw, transfer, or retire.

### `/collaborate` — How work ends

A relationship is not closed merely because meetings stop or a deliverable is
sent. Closure records remaining defects and obligations; finalizes
contribution, credit, and review; resolves data and material custody; declares
publication and correction status; assigns support and repair; and names the
stewardship, transfer, archive, or retirement path.

### `/collaborate` or `/work` — Human-AI collaboration

The durable artifact, not the conversation, is the shared work surface. AI may
search, compare, draft, code, criticize, and transform bounded material.
Sources, inference, uncertainty, contradiction, review, and replacement remain
inspectable. Production does not certify itself, criticism does not silently
rewrite what it judges, and consequential promotion remains a declared human
or institutional act.

### `/about` — Authority

Responsibility may be distributed, but it may not be dissolved. Authority is
bounded by competence, consequence, and repair capacity; expertise in one
domain does not silently transfer to another. The person or body named as
responsible must be able to inspect the evidence, authorize correction, and
route unresolved consequences.

### `/about` — Standing

Collective voice should not manufacture consensus. Consequential uses of
"we" should make the speaker, participants, authority, represented parties,
material dissent, affected nonparticipants, and review path legible.
Participation does not by itself imply consent, endorsement, authorship, or
institutional authority.

### `/about` — Repair

Repair is an institutional capability, not an emergency promise. A working
repair route has an owner, authority, evidence access, resources, and a target
state. Depending on the consequence, it may require correction, appeal,
rollback, compensation, reassignment, redesign, preserved lessons, or a
decision to stop.

### `/about` — Continuity and self-application

Founder provenance should remain visible without becoming permanent final
authority. The lab's intended continuity mechanisms include role-bounded
authority, independent criticism, contribution records, conflict and appeal
paths, recusal where founder interests are directly involved, and archives and
decision rules that remain usable without founder memory.

**Required label:** Proposed institutional mechanisms; operating evidence
should be linked as it becomes available.

### `/business` — replacement positioning

**Headline:** Refactor the system before scaling its defects.

Boundary First Labs helps organizations recover the actual domain behind a
workflow, identify the distinctions and obligations that must survive change,
map where authority and consequence have separated, and design a bounded,
observable intervention with explicit failure, repair, and retirement paths.

This is collaborative diagnosis, not borrowed certainty. Domain expertise
remains with the people who hold it; recommendations state their evidence,
limits, decision owner, and next test.

### Guided first passage — institutional scene support

Claims and collaborations become durable only when their authority,
provenance, criticism, dissent, promotion, correction, and stewardship remain
visible. The institution is part of the system it evaluates, so its own
decisions must expose the same boundaries and repair paths.

### Audience router — Partner

Bring a bounded problem, source set, practice, or testing environment. We will
clarify what each party contributes, who may decide and publish, what evidence
would change the work, and how the relationship can conclude without erasing
credit, disagreement, or remaining obligations.

### Audience router — Critic

Criticism is not opposition to the mission; it is institutional maintenance.
Reviewers should be able to identify unsupported claims, hidden assumptions,
conflicts, missing affected parties, and closure defects without being required
to silently repair the work they judge or endorse the larger program.

## Editorial decision

Do not create more abstract institutional maxims in the next content pass.
The corpus already has enough. The publication-quality opportunity is to make
the doctrine operationally legible:

```text
principle
    -> declared scope
    -> named role and authority
    -> evidence and dissent
    -> decision gate
    -> repair owner
    -> stewardship or closure
    -> public operating record
```

That sequence gives the website substance without pretending the institution
is more mature than its evidence.
