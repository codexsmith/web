# Tasks and Goals

## Program objective

Build a credible Boundary First Labs research and public-use lane that connects social lawfulness, economic consequence, digital governance, scientific inquiry, and educational access through one explicit systems grammar.

---

# Goals

## G1 — Canonicalize the social-lawful lane

**Outcome:** A stable doctrine linking authority, consequence, representation, contestability, and repair.

**Success evidence:**

- canonical definitions accepted into the corpus;
- relation to Distinction Theory and Boundary Theory is explicit;
- normative axioms are separated from mechanical claims;
- at least two external reviewers can apply the audit without founder interpretation.

## G2 — Build consequence accounting as the first constructive instrument

**Outcome:** A ledger and case-study method that replaces “externality” with attributable consequence and repair liability.

**Success evidence:**

- completed ledger schema;
- one worked business case;
- one public-sector or infrastructure case;
- comparison with existing accounting, ESG, lifecycle, tort, and impact frameworks;
- documented differences and added value.

## G3 — Formalize agency laundering and digital governance

**Outcome:** A traceable model of originating agency, intermediary composition, control, benefit, foreseeability, and repair.

**Success evidence:**

- stable definition;
- graph or typed trace representation;
- three platform or AI case studies;
- legal limiting principles;
- civil-liberties review.

## G4 — Resolve the anonymity/provenance architecture as a maintained dilemma

**Outcome:** A design paper that protects vulnerable users while making consequential organized power legible.

**Success evidence:**

- threat models for user, platform, corporation, hostile state, and regulator;
- privacy-preserving design options;
- explicit due-process and abuse-resistance requirements;
- review by privacy and civil-rights specialists.

## G5 — Establish Boundary-First scientific inquiry as a method

**Outcome:** A concise method specification and comparative paper showing where it refines existing scientific and systems practices.

**Success evidence:**

- standardized inquiry worksheet;
- three case studies in different domains;
- explicit comparison to systems engineering, design science, operationalization, model validation, and philosophy of science;
- clearly measured improvement in problem formulation or defect detection.

## G6 — Build the public on-ramp curriculum

**Outcome:** A simple, democratic route from ordinary intuition into formal inquiry.

**Success evidence:**

- five flagship modules;
- classroom/workshop pilot;
- comprehension feedback;
- metaphor firewalls;
- bridge into at least three disciplinary formalisms.

## G7 — Publish the founder/provenance narrative without making biography carry validation

**Outcome:** A credible public narrative explaining the theory’s form, the double-interface origin, and the artificer posture.

**Success evidence:**

- one polished essay;
- clear distinction between provenance, motivation, theory, and evidence;
- external editorial review for tone and grandiosity risk;
- integration into the Boundary First Labs “Start Here” experience.

## G8 — Create an external criticism and validation loop

**Outcome:** The framework encounters agents who do not share its grammar.

**Success evidence:**

- multidisciplinary review panel;
- critique ledger;
- public revision notes;
- at least one documented case where the framework is narrowed or rejected.

# Dependencies

- Historical and economic public claims require sourcing before release.
- Legal claims require jurisdiction-specific review.
- Anonymity architecture requires privacy and civil-liberties participation.
- Scientific-method claims require comparison to established methods literature.
- Founder narrative should be published after or alongside concrete instruments, not as the sole proof surface.

# Stop conditions

Do not promote a claim when:

- provenance is missing;
- the domain mapping is only metaphorical but presented as formal;
- a normative axiom is presented as deduction;
- the claim implies solved mathematics or validated physics without required proof;
- a governance proposal cannot survive the hostile-regulator test;
- affected parties have no standing or repair route;
- the method cannot state where it fails.
