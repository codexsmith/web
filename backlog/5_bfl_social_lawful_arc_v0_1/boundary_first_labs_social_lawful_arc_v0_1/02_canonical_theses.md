# Canonical Thesis Register

This document records the conversation’s strongest formulations in a form suitable for corpus review.

## A. Social lawfulness

### T-SL-01 — Lawfulness/coercion distinction

**Status:** [Conversation-derived]  
**Claim:** Lawful structure and coercive structure are not equivalent. Lawfulness requires explicit authority, bounded power, representation, consequence return, contestability, and repair.

### T-SL-02 — Disorder is not freedom

**Status:** [Normative axiom + systems claim]  
**Claim:** Ambiguous or weakly specified social structure does not eliminate power; it often allows power to operate without accountability.

### T-SL-03 — Social too-big-to-fail

**Status:** [Conversation-derived]  
**Claim:** Institutions defended as too large or embedded to replace exhibit concentrated dependency and systemic fragility, not resilience.

## B. Economic mechanics

### T-EC-01 — Money as transition capacity

**Status:** [Formal candidate]  
**Claim:** Money functions as a transport medium for reachable economic state transitions.

### T-EC-02 — Edge-circuit necessity

**Status:** [Empirical candidate]  
**Claim:** Households and local businesses require continuous circulation to reproduce ordinary operation, while concentrated capital can remain dormant or recursively financialized for longer periods.

### T-EC-03 — Concentration field effect

**Status:** [Formal and empirical candidate]  
**Claim:** Concentrated purchasing power changes the price environment and production target function experienced by agents who do not possess that wealth.

### T-EC-04 — Second-order inequality

**Status:** [Conversation-derived]  
**Claim:** Inequality becomes second-order when those with greater resources reshape the environment so that lower resource levels become increasingly disabling.

### T-EC-05 — Externality reclassification

**Status:** [Conversation-derived]  
**Claim:** An externality is an unbooked consequence produced by an incomplete accounting boundary.

### T-EC-06 — Profit closure

**Status:** [Formal candidate]  
**Claim:** Reported profit is not closed profit when foreseeable displaced consequences and unfunded repair liabilities remain outside the ledger.

## C. Business agentics

### T-BA-01 — Objective inadmissibility

**Status:** [Normative axiom + systems claim]  
**Claim:** Unbounded shareholder-return maximization is an inadmissible terminal objective for a business agent embedded in human and ecological systems.

### T-BA-02 — Engineering toward failure

**Status:** [Empirical candidate]  
**Claim:** Under cost-minimizing, return-maximizing pressure and weak market exit, firms are incentivized to reduce durability and repairability toward the minimum saleable boundary.

### T-BA-03 — Obligation outlives utility

**Status:** [Empirical candidate]  
**Claim:** Long-duration financing can create obligations more durable than the useful life of the financed product, transferring engineering and market risk to the household.

## D. Digital governance

### T-DG-01 — Communication as social substrate

**Status:** [Conversation-derived]  
**Claim:** The internet mediates the transport layer through which coordination, exchange, identity, knowledge, and political action occur; it therefore cannot be governed as a neutral message conduit alone.

### T-DG-02 — Agency laundering

**Status:** [Definition candidate]  
**Claim:** Networked systems allow originating agency to be distributed through intermediaries while preserving consequence and fragmenting responsibility.

### T-DG-03 — Local admissibility/global inadmissibility

**Status:** [Formal candidate]  
**Claim:** Individually permissible actions can compose into a globally inadmissible system.

### T-DG-04 — Private governance

**Status:** [Empirical and legal candidate]  
**Claim:** Platform terms, ranking systems, moderation, identity controls, and recommendation algorithms perform quasi-governance functions without equivalent public-law constraints.

## E. Anonymity and provenance

### T-AP-01 — Protected identity/unaccountable power distinction

**Status:** [Conversation-derived]  
**Claim:** Privacy, pseudonymity, anonymity, untraceability, and impunity must not be treated as equivalent.

### T-AP-02 — Power-provenance proportionality

**Status:** [Design principle]  
**Claim:** Accountability requirements should increase with reach, automation, coordination, and foreseeable consequence rather than with mere ordinary expression.

### T-AP-03 — Maintained dilemma

**Status:** [Normative constraint]  
**Claim:** Digital systems must simultaneously protect vulnerable speakers from exposure and protect the public from unaccountable organized power.

## F. Scientific method and pedagogy

### T-SM-01 — Boundary-First scientific refinement

**Status:** [Programmatic claim]  
**Claim:** Boundary-First refines scientific inquiry by making system boundaries, distinctions, pressures, invariants, defects, and repair paths explicit before and through testing.

### T-SM-02 — Common grammar as on-ramp

**Status:** [Programmatic claim]  
**Claim:** Primitive systems terms can lower the conceptual barrier to formal inquiry without lowering proof or evidence standards.

### T-SM-03 — Traction claim

**Status:** [Claim ceiling]  
**Claim:** Boundary-First methods have produced structural traction, candidate invariants, decompositions, and testable research directions on large problems; they have not yet established complete solutions merely by doing so.

## G. Provenance and form

### T-PF-01 — Constitutional provenance

**Status:** [Interpretive claim]  
**Claim:** Because the theory arose from failures of representation and repair, contestability and consequence-linked responsibility are constitutive requirements of faithful implementation.

### T-PF-02 — No sovereign representation

**Status:** [Normative axiom]  
**Claim:** No representation may become sovereign over the represented; maps, institutions, models, and governing mechanisms must remain answerable to the territory and people they organize.

### T-PF-03 — Earned return

**Status:** [Formal candidate]  
**Claim:** Vicious circularity assumes its conclusion; admissive or circummissive return passes through declared transformations and earns identity by preserving a specified invariant or equivalence.
