## Why Boundary First?

Complex systems rarely fail because no one had enough information.

They fail because important information stopped being represented.

Context disappears.

Assumptions become implicit.

Ownership becomes ambiguous.

Consequences move outside the accounting boundary.

Interfaces preserve syntax while losing meaning.

People leave and take institutional state with them.

Models become more sophisticated while their original assumptions become less visible.

Boundary First begins one step earlier.

Before asking how to optimize a system, we ask:

**What is the system?**

**What distinguishes one state from another?**

**Where are its actual boundaries?**

**What information must cross those boundaries?**

**What must remain invariant when the representation changes?**

**What consequences are being generated, and where do they go?**

This discipline began in consequential software engineering and has expanded into a broader research program.

# Research

## Boundary Theory

Boundary Theory studies what happens when boundaries are treated as active components of systems rather than passive edges.

A boundary determines more than where something stops.

It can determine:

* admissibility
* interaction
* responsibility
* information flow
* transformation
* observability
* consequence
* closure

The broader Boundary First research program asks what becomes visible when these structures are represented explicitly.

---

## The Cut

Our current foundational approach begins with the **cut** as a primitive distinction operation.

A cut separates a context into distinguishable regimes:

[
A \mid B
]

Topology then gives a rigorous language for studying where that distinction remains active.

For a subset (A) of a topological space (X),

[
\partial A
==========

\overline{A}\cap\overline{X\setminus A}.
]

In this interpretation:

**The cut introduces distinction.**

**The boundary is the topological locus where that distinction remains locally unresolved.**

This provides a minimal starting point from which richer representational grammars can be constructed.

---

# Distinction Spaces

A Distinction Space is a context equipped with an admissible family of distinctions and the relations among them.

Rather than beginning by assuming a state space, we ask:

**What distinctions are necessary for that state space to become representable in the first place?**

This reverses a common modeling assumption:

[
\text{distinction grammar}
\rightarrow
\text{admissible state space}
\rightarrow
\text{measure}
\rightarrow
\text{dynamics}
\rightarrow
\text{emergence}.
]

The objective is not to replace existing mathematics.

It is to expose the representational assumptions that existing mathematics often carries implicitly.

# Schemathematics

## What is Schemathematics?

**Schemathematics is the study of the minimum schema required to faithfully represent a mathematical regime.**

It interprets mathematics through a discipline familiar to computer science and software engineering:

> What information must be made explicit so that another competent system can reconstruct the same regime without depending on hidden state?

For a mathematical regime (\mathcal M), we seek a minimal sufficient schema

[
\Sigma_{\min}(\mathcal M)
]

containing the distinctions necessary to reconstruct its:

* entities
* relations
* admissibility rules
* transformations
* invariants
* boundary conditions
* closure rules

The core research question is:

> **What is the smallest representational grammar from which this mathematics remains faithfully reconstructible?**

## Why Computer Science?

Computers cannot rely on “you know what I meant.”

Software engineering therefore forces assumptions into explicit structures:

* schemas
* types
* interfaces
* constraints
* state
* invariants
* serialization
* migration
* validation
* dependency
* recovery
* observability

Schemathematics applies that representational discipline to mathematics itself.

The goal is not to turn mathematics into software.

The goal is to ask mathematical systems the same question good software architecture asks:

> **What information must be preserved for the system to remain itself?**

# Contexture

A **Contexture** is a locally coherent grammatical closure over a Distinction Space.

Provisionally, a Contexture contains enough structure to specify:

* what may be distinguished
* which roles distinguished things may occupy
* which configurations are admissible
* which transformations are permitted
* which invariants must survive those transformations
* where closure succeeds
* where defects occur
* when the grammar itself must be extended

This makes the grammar of a mathematical regime an explicit research object.

---

## Category Theory as a Contexture

One current line of research interprets ordinary category theory as operating within a predefined relational grammar.

The basic categorical cell

[
A\xrightarrow{f}B
]

already distinguishes several functional roles:

* context
* source
* transport
* target

Category theory then studies identity, composition, and coherence while that grammar remains fixed.

Schemathematics asks a prior question:

> **What made those roles available as mathematical distinctions in the first place?**

Under this interpretation, categorical structure becomes one particularly powerful fixed grammatical regime inside a more general Contexture framework.

This is an active research direction, not a claimed replacement for category theory.

---

# Representational Mechanics

Representational Mechanics studies what happens when a structure changes representation.

Given two representations of a structure,

[
\rho_A(X)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\rho_B(X),
]

we ask:

* What transformation connects them?
* What must survive that transformation?
* What information is lost?
* What new distinctions become necessary?
* When does the transport fail?
* Is the failure in the object, the transformation, or the representational grammar itself?

The central object is not merely the representation.

It is the **admissible transport between representations**.

---

# The Atlas

A sufficiently rich system may require multiple locally coherent Contextures.

An atlas records those regimes and the admissible transports among them.

Each local chart answers:

> What distinctions can be represented coherently here?

Each transition answers:

> What can be transported into another representation?

Each invariant answers:

> What must remain unchanged?

Each defect answers:

> What failed to cross?

The long-term objective is a mathematical language capable of representing not only objects and transformations, but changes in the grammars through which those objects become intelligible.

---

# Proof and Verification Research

Schemathematics naturally suggests a new architecture for machine-assisted mathematics.

A mathematical regime can be treated as:

[
\text{regime}
\rightarrow
\text{minimal schema}
\rightarrow
\text{machine-readable representation}
\rightarrow
\text{proof obligations}
\rightarrow
\text{proof certificate}
\rightarrow
\text{trusted checker}.
]

This suggests research directions including:

* machine-readable mathematical schemas
* mathematical intermediate representations
* automated assumption extraction
* proof checking
* proof search
* theorem transport
* cross-system formalization
* automatic counterexample generation
* schema comparison
* detection of missing primitive distinctions

One particularly important possibility is distinguishing:

[
\text{the proposed result is invalid}
]

from:

[
\text{the current representational grammar is insufficient}.
]

That distinction could provide a useful new layer for mathematical discovery systems.

# Current Research Questions

Boundary First Labs is actively investigating questions including:

### Minimal Mathematical Representation

What is the minimum schema required to faithfully reconstruct a mathematical regime without relying on hidden context?

### Contexture

Can mathematical grammars themselves be represented as extensible formal objects?

### Category and Grammar

Can ordinary categorical structure be recovered as a fixed grammatical regime inside a more general Contexture framework?

### Mathematical Intermediate Representation

Can different mathematical languages be translated through a common intermediate representation while preserving explicitly declared invariants?

### Automated Proof Infrastructure

Can proof obligations and verification rules be generated directly from a regime's schema?

### Representational Defect

Can repeated failures of closure identify a missing primitive distinction rather than merely a missing proof step?

### Boundary-First Statistical Mechanics

Can admissible state spaces be generated from primitive distinction and boundary structures rather than assumed in advance?

### Hard Mathematical Benchmarks

Can Schemathematical representations produce useful new approaches to independently difficult mathematical problems?

Millennium Prize Problems are being considered as adversarial benchmark environments for this machinery.

A benchmark is not a claim of solution.

### Foundational Physics

Can boundary-generated state spaces and statistical closure constrain physical regimes, dimensionless couplings, or relationships among existing theories?

The fine-structure constant and unification remain long-horizon research targets.

These are research questions, not established results.

# Research Claim Discipline

Boundary First Labs distinguishes carefully between levels of evidence.

Research materials should be labeled using categories such as:

**Established**
Standard mathematics, published science, or externally verified fact.

**Reformulation**
Existing structure expressed in Boundary First or Schemathematical language.

**Derived**
A result that follows from declared assumptions and prior established structure.

**Computational**
A result produced or supported by explicit computation.

**Conjecture**
A mathematically motivated claim that has not been proven.

**Open Question**
A research direction for which no result is being claimed.

**Speculative**
A deliberately exploratory idea included to expose possible structure.

The objective is not to make ambitious questions smaller.

It is to make the boundary between **what is known and what is being tested** explicit.

# Founder

Boundary First Labs was founded by a Georgia Tech-trained software engineer and independent researcher whose work has crossed artificial intelligence, software systems, public research, insurance, shipping, scientific measurement, machine-learning R&D, medical systems, government constraints, and both legacy and greenfield software.

Repeatedly entering and leaving consequential systems exposed a recurring failure pattern:

organizations often retained the first-order artifact while losing the second-order context required to understand it.

The code remained.

The assumptions disappeared.

The process remained.

The rationale disappeared.

The metric remained.

The consequence disappeared from the representation.

That experience led to a long independent research program spanning language, systems theory, mathematics, mathematical physics, computation, governance, and representation.

AI became an important part of the final stage of that work by making cross-disciplinary knowledge traversal possible at a scale that would ordinarily require substantial institutional support.

From the outside, the three foundings looked different.

**understand the system, expose the hidden structure, and build a representation capable of carrying it forward.**

# Institutional Philosophy

**Complexity should not be allowed to erase the basics.**

* what the objects are
* what distinguishes them
* why the distinctions matter
* what the interfaces mean
* what assumptions are active
* who owns the consequences

# The Oathbound

Boundary First Labs also carries a broader ethical principle.

Consequences do not disappear because a representation fails to record them.

When consequences are hidden, diffuse, externalized, or difficult to describe, the responsibility is not merely to witness them.

It is to translate them into forms that other people can perceive, verify, understand, and act upon without falsifying the underlying causal structure.

The objective is not control.

It is not forced conformity.

It is the construction of systems capable of **lawfully redirecting, absorbing, exposing, distributing, and repairing consequence while preserving meaningful agency**.

In short:

**Keep account.
Make the account legible.
Build somewhere truthful for the consequence to go.**

# Core Principles

## Preserve the basics under composition.

Complexity does not excuse losing the assumptions that made the system coherent.

## Preserve interpretive conditions under transmission.

A message is not complete if its receiver cannot reconstruct what makes it meaningful.

## Do not corrupt the data to satisfy a bad schema.

Sometimes the representation is the thing that needs to change.

## Hidden state is still state.

If continuation depends on knowledge that has not been represented, the system is incomplete.

## Preserve agency. Account for consequence.

Good governance does not require controlling every action. It requires making consequence visible, attributable, and repairable.

## Another competent witness should be able to reconstruct the system.

If they cannot, important context has not been preserved.
