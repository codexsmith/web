# People Are Not Overhead: Review Worksheet

This worksheet turns [People Make the Value. People Are Not Overhead.](/artifact/people-make-the-value-people-are-not-overhead) into a bounded institutional review.

It is designed for one decision, workflow, service, automation, vendor relationship, operating change, or product slice. It is not an organization-wide moral score. Its purpose is to find where value and consequence have been represented asymmetrically, identify the people and capacities carrying the difference, and specify a repair that can be owned and verified.

## Before beginning

Name the review boundary:

```text
Decision, workflow, or service under review:
Why it is being reviewed:
Institutional owner:
Affected period:
Included systems and organizations:
Known exclusions:
People represented in the review:
People not yet represented:
Review facilitator:
Decision and repair authority:
```

Do not begin with a score. Begin with a claim.

```text
The institution currently claims that this decision or workflow creates:

The evidence offered for that claim is:

The declared operating boundary is:

The result would be materially false or incomplete if:
```

## Evidence discipline

For every material answer, record whether it is supported by:

- operating records or telemetry;
- financial or staffing records;
- policy, contract, or system documentation;
- direct observation;
- affected-party testimony;
- management interpretation;
- an illustrative assumption requiring validation;
- missing or inaccessible evidence.

Affected-party testimony is evidence of experienced consequence. It should not be dismissed because it is qualitative, nor automatically generalized beyond its scope. Institutional records are also bounded representations; they should not be treated as complete merely because they are numerical.

## Module 1 — Value source and destination

Identify the value the institution claims, who makes and sustains it, and who receives usable benefit.

```text
Claimed value:
People whose labor, judgment, care, maintenance, or participation creates it:
Tools, infrastructure, capital, ecological systems, and inherited knowledge it depends on:
Direct recipients:
Affected nonrecipients and noncustomers:
Evidence that the value reaches its stated destination:
```

Red flags:

- value is named only as revenue, throughput, valuation, or internal activity;
- recipients are represented while producers, maintainers, or affected noncustomers are absent;
- the stated benefit has no observable return path to people or durable system capacity.

Repair direction: add a value source-destination map and name the evidence by which the claimed benefit returns to its human or public terminus.

## Module 2 — Human capacity consumed and preserved

Trace the capacities required to make the result possible.

```text
Attention and recovery required:
Physical and mental load:
Judgment and discretion required:
Tacit knowledge or institutional memory used:
Training, care, accommodation, and support required:
Trust and cooperative willingness used:
Capacity built or preserved:
Capacity depleted or made fragile:
```

Red flags:

- headcount or time reduction is counted as value without capability analysis;
- recovery, accommodation, training, or maintenance is treated as optional despite operational dependence;
- the same people repeatedly absorb volatility while the process appears stable.

Repair direction: establish a human-capacity ledger that records which capacities are consumed, preserved, regenerated, or transferred.

## Module 3 — Hidden human shock absorbers

Find the people who make an incomplete system appear complete.

```text
Who handles exceptions?
Who reconciles incompatible records?
Who explains confusing policy to affected people?
Who catches automation or classification errors?
Who absorbs customer distress or emotional consequence?
Who performs unpaid, unplanned, or unowned maintenance?
What fails if this hidden work stops?
```

Red flags:

- “automated” work depends on invisible manual correction;
- exceptions are handled through personal memory or goodwill;
- operational success requires work that has no role, time, budget, or escalation route.

Repair direction: represent and support the work, redesign the defect that creates it, transfer it to an accountable role, or stop the dependent process.

## Module 4 — Externalized cost and repair

Trace burdens that leave the boundary reporting the gain.

```text
Cost or risk reduced inside the reviewed boundary:
Household burden created or increased:
Worker or contractor burden created or increased:
Customer or user burden created or increased:
Public-system or community burden created or increased:
Ecological or infrastructural burden created or increased:
Future maintenance or repair obligation created:
Who currently pays, waits, adapts, or repairs?
```

Red flags:

- a local saving is reported while downstream cleanup is unmeasured;
- the party receiving the benefit can exit before repair becomes due;
- those carrying the burden lack standing in the decision.

Repair direction: expand the consequence boundary, assign the repair obligation, and revise the value claim to include transferred cost.

## Module 5 — Delegated agency depth

Map every institutional or automated layer between human purpose and final consequence.

```text
Original human or public purpose:
Institution holding primary authority:
Vendors, platforms, contractors, agents, and intermediaries:
Authority transferred at each step:
Evidence returned at each step:
People who ultimately benefit:
People who ultimately bear failure:
Layer able to interrupt the chain:
Terminal accountability holder:
```

Red flags:

- each organization satisfies its client while nobody represents affected people;
- responsibility fragments across contracts, platforms, or automated systems;
- no layer can explain the complete path from delegated purpose to human consequence;
- a vendor increases the client’s capability without representing harmful uses or affected noncustomers.

Repair direction: create a Delegated Agency Return Map with purpose, authority, interruption, consequence, and repair paths for every consequential layer.

## Module 6 — Standing and contestability

Determine whether affected people can understand, challenge, and correct the system.

```text
Who receives an explanation?
Who can inspect relevant evidence?
Who may challenge a decision or classification?
What support is available to exercise that right?
Who hears the challenge?
What can that role change?
How is correction communicated and propagated?
```

Red flags:

- an appeal exists formally but is inaccessible in time, language, cost, or authority;
- affected people must prove internal system errors without access to relevant evidence;
- empathy or executive intervention substitutes for a durable correction route.

Repair direction: add explanation, evidence access, assisted contest, bounded adjudication authority, and a correction path that updates dependent records and systems.

## Module 7 — Responsibility and repair capacity

Name the repair owner and test whether responsibility is matched by real capacity.

```text
Material discrepancy or burden:
Named repair owner:
Authority available to that owner:
Information and expertise available:
Time and resources available:
Dependencies and approvals:
Escalation route:
Verification method:
Residual risk and acceptance authority:
```

Red flags:

- the named owner can observe but cannot change the responsible boundary;
- a team carries accountability without time, funding, evidence, or authority;
- repair is deferred without a recorded obligation, date, or risk holder.

Repair direction: align authority, knowledge, time, resources, and obligation; otherwise escalate responsibility to the boundary capable of closure.

## Module 8 — Local savings and global depletion

Test whether the claimed improvement preserves the larger systems on which it depends.

```text
Local metric improved:
Boundary reporting the improvement:
Larger capacities the improvement depends on:
Near-term depletion or fragility introduced:
Long-term depletion or option loss introduced:
Counterevidence or beneficial effects:
Conditions under which the gain remains valid:
Conditions that should reopen the decision:
```

Red flags:

- success requires repeated depletion that cannot be regenerated;
- the metric improves while service quality, trust, safety, accessibility, or maintainability declines;
- future repair is assumed but neither funded nor assigned.

Repair direction: add a local-global closure test and define the capacities and future options that must remain invariant.

## Classifying findings without a moral score

Classify each finding by its current evidentiary state:

- **Represented and supported** — the value, burden, standing, and repair path are visible and evidence-backed.
- **Potential gap** — a plausible consequence is identified but evidence is incomplete.
- **Material displaced consequence** — evidence shows a significant burden outside the reporting or decision boundary.
- **Unowned repair** — a material discrepancy exists without aligned responsibility and capacity.
- **Insufficient evidence** — the review cannot responsibly determine the condition.
- **Contested interpretation** — relevant parties or evidence support materially different accounts that require adjudication.

Do not collapse these categories into one numeric grade. A high aggregate score could hide one severe unowned consequence, while a low score could overstate uncertainty as demonstrated harm.

## Repair record

Every material finding should end in a proposed state change:

```text
Finding:
Evidence and limitations:
People and systems affected:
Responsible boundary:
Proposed repair:
Repair owner:
Required authority and resources:
Expected consequence:
Verification channel:
Target date:
Residual risk:
Reopen condition:
```

A review is incomplete when it produces only criticism, awareness, or a score. It should produce a repair route, a named evidence need, an explicit risk acceptance, or an escalation packet.

## Self-assessment and independent review

Teams may use this worksheet for self-assessment when the scope is low-risk and the purpose is discovery. Independent facilitation becomes more important when:

- affected people have less power than the decision-makers;
- employment, health, safety, housing, eligibility, accessibility, livelihood, or public rights are implicated;
- the institution benefits from a narrow interpretation of the boundary;
- records are disputed or inaccessible;
- retaliation or confidentiality concerns constrain testimony;
- the result will be used publicly, contractually, or for certification.

This worksheet is not a substitute for professional accounting, legal, labor, safety, accessibility, or human-subjects expertise.

## Present claim ceiling and pilot path

This is a **candidate instrument, version 0.1**. It has not been validated as an audit standard, certification, compliance tool, or comparative score. Its terminology and modules require review by accounting, labor, disability, care, organizational-design, governance, and affected-party perspectives.

The next responsible step is to pilot the worksheet in three bounded settings: one software or automation workflow, one public or service process, and one B2B vendor relationship. Each pilot should record which questions exposed actionable information, which created ambiguity or burden, what repair followed, and what the instrument failed to represent.
